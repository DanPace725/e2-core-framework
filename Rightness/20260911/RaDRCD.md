## Proposal: Rightness as Distributed Relational Closure Dynamics

### 1. Core proposition

Rightness is provisionally understood not as a single property, scalar quantity, rule, or terminal moral value, but as a **complex relational configuration that emerges within and between systems capable of maintaining distinctions among possible states**.

Such systems do not encounter all configurations as equivalent. They exhibit gradients of preference, aversion, viability, fit, coherence, flourishing, threat, obligation, correctness, and other forms of non-equivalence. At sufficiently complex levels these gradients become perceptible, representable, communicable, contestable, inherited, revised, and deliberately acted upon.

Rightness concerns the organization, adjudication, and resolution of those non-equivalences.

It is therefore neither merely subjective nor straightforwardly objective. It arises within relational fields whose participants are embedded, differently situated, differently capable, and differently affected. Any actual judgment of rightness occurs under finite frame, inheritance, scale, scope, and resolution rather than from a view outside the system.

The earlier essay already approached rightness as emergent, contextual, negotiated, dynamic, metastable, and increasingly complex as agency and abstraction increase, while explicitly denying that stability alone establishes rightness. 

### 2. Four objects should remain distinct

A useful working distinction is:

**Configuration**: the actual relational arrangement being evaluated, including its participants, states, constraints, possibilities, histories, consequences, and couplings.

**Closure**: the transition by which a system ceases to hold some relevant possibilities open and commits toward one interpretation, classification, action, or state.

**Verdict**: the representational or behavioral result deposited by that closure: *safe*, *dangerous*, *correct*, *unjust*, *acceptable*, *right*, *wrong*, and so on.

**Adequacy**: how well the closure and verdict actually fit the larger relational configuration they purport to resolve.

These may occur so closely in time that they appear identical. Biological aversion to rotten food can proceed from perception to closure to bodily and behavioral verdict within fractions of a second. At coarse resolution, “the body rejected the apple” is sufficient. At finer resolution, sensory localization, accumulated salience, recognition, valence, threshold crossing, physiological response, motor preparation, facial signaling, and explicit judgment are distinguishable stages.

Their distinction is therefore functional rather than dependent on large temporal separation.

A closure is the **transition into commitment**.

A verdict is what that transition **deposits**.

Adequacy is not identical to either.

### 3. Closure is thresholded and dynamical

Normative judgment should not presently be modeled as a static function in which several independent variables are inserted and a rightness score emerges.

A more plausible model is dynamical.

Information, constraints, prior states, attention, affect, memory, social signals, and environmental perturbations alter an evolving evaluative configuration. As relevant gradients accumulate, the system approaches one or more boundaries. Crossing a critical boundary permits or causes closure into a metastable basin.

Very abstractly:

\[
\text{configuration}
\rightarrow
\text{localization}
\rightarrow
\text{gradient formation}
\rightarrow
\text{threshold}
\rightarrow
\text{closure}
\rightarrow
\text{verdict}
\]

The verdict then becomes part of the next configuration.

This creates path dependence. A prior judgment changes attention, expectations, available interpretations, and the amount of evidence required for reversal. Entry and exit thresholds therefore need not be symmetrical. In familiar dynamical language, normative and perceptual closure may display **hysteresis**.

This gives a structural explanation for the stickiness of judgments, reputations, doctrines, norms, prejudices, commitments, and institutions without assuming that their persistence establishes their correctness.

The existing CFA/CFAR family is already structurally compatible with this picture. It treats constraint, fluctuation, attention, and resolution as coupled dynamical quantities rather than independent inputs to a simple score.  TCL likewise supplies an existing vocabulary of temporal coupling, thresholds, latency, metabolic cost, and constraint lamination. 

### 4. Warrant is indexed, local, and transportable only under conditions

Warrant should not be treated as equivalent to truth, correctness, adequacy, or rightness.

A provisional definition is:

> **Warrant describes the degree to which the relational grounds available to an observer, at a given slice and under relevant stakes, license a particular closure.**

This makes warrant observer-indexed without reducing it to whim.

A biological system may be warranted in rejecting food that smells spoiled even when later analysis reveals the food was safe. The organism possessed limited information, faced asymmetric consequences, and closed according to relationships constitutive of its position in the field.

The closure can therefore be:

locally warranted,  
factually mistaken,  
and pragmatically appropriate.

Likewise, a strong fear response can have a causal history without necessarily being warranted by its present configuration. Warrant requires more than “the system did this.” The available grounds must bear some adequate relation to the closure under the relevant conditions.

This distinction becomes especially important across abstraction layers.

A lower layer may close:

> danger.

A higher cognitive layer may subsequently represent that verdict itself and evaluate it:

> My body is producing a danger verdict, but the object is harmless.

The later verdict does not necessarily retroactively erase the local warrant of the earlier one. Instead, the question becomes whether that warrant **survives transport into the wider slice**.

Thus:

\[
W(V\mid s_0)
\]

does not entail:

\[
W(V\mid s_1)
\]

when \(s_1\) contains additional relevant relations unavailable under \(s_0\).

This seems especially compatible with E²'s broader discipline against allowing local resolution to masquerade as universal resolution.

### 5. Adequacy does not require an observer, but assessment of adequacy does

A verdict can be inadequate whether or not anyone recognizes the failure.

If a person judges an apple safe, eats it, and becomes ill, the verdict had some inadequacy relative to the configuration even if the cause is never discovered.

Therefore:

**adequacy** is a relation between closure/verdict and what actually follows or obtains.

**assessment of adequacy** is another observer-indexed evaluative process.

Once a system can represent its own prior verdicts, those verdicts themselves become candidate configurations for further adjudication.

That produces recursive structure:

\[
C_0 \rightarrow K_0 \rightarrow V_0
\]

followed by:

\[
(C_0,V_0,G_1) \rightarrow K_1 \rightarrow V_1
\]

where new grounds \(G_1\) may affirm, qualify, or contest the earlier verdict.

This is not necessarily an infinite tower requiring a final transcendent judge. It is the capacity of sufficiently complex systems to **reopen prior closure**.

Biological, affective, cognitive, interpersonal, institutional, and cultural adjudication may therefore instantiate related closure grammars while operating with different timescales, thresholds, memories, authorities, and available forms of evidence.

Higher layers are constituted through lower ones without being required to reproduce their conclusions.

**Substrate dependence does not imply verdict dependence.**

### 6. Rightness is distributed

Any sufficiently complex configuration may be encountered from multiple legitimate relational positions.

An employee may encounter a company as generous.

A neighboring community may encounter it as polluting.

A regulator may encounter it as compliant.

An investor may encounter it as efficient.

A future population may inherit consequences unavailable to any present observer.

These are not necessarily competing opinions about an otherwise fully visible object. They can be **different localizations of a distributed configuration**.

Accordingly, no observer should be presumed to possess the fully resolved rightness of the whole.

The mistake occurs when a warranted local closure is transported beyond the domain that supports it:

> “This employer treats me well”

becomes

> “This is a good company.”

The first may be highly warranted.

The second requires considerably more.

This means the burden for a rightness verdict should generally increase with the scope of its claim. A biological avoidance response can legitimately close quickly. A societal judgment involving millions of people, long temporal horizons, unequal power, and distributed consequences cannot claim equivalent warrant from equally narrow evidence.

Rightness at larger scales therefore becomes increasingly dependent upon localization, participation, cross-perspective integration, preservation of remainder, and the possibility of revision.

### 7. The semantic cluster reflects structure, not merely lexical ambiguity

Terms such as *correct*, *justified*, *warranted*, *just*, *proper*, *fitting*, *legitimate*, *moral*, *ethical*, and perhaps *good* are related to rightness but are not interchangeable.

A belief can be justified yet incorrect.

A conclusion can be correct while reached improperly.

A verdict can be warranted but incomplete.

An authority can possess legitimate jurisdiction and still reach a mistaken result.

The relation therefore appears not to be:

\[
Right = Just = Correct = Warranted = Proper
\]

Instead, these concepts may expose different relational aspects of a larger evaluative configuration.

Rightness may be what appears when these partially independent and partially coupled forms of adequacy are considered together at an appropriate slice.

This is consistent with the earlier intuition that the semantic breadth of *right* may reflect a high-dimensional referent rather than merely semantic drift. The essay itself describes rightness as a “multi-axis, perhaps multidimensional operator or function” and later as a complex relational system. 

### 8. These dimensions should not presently be modeled as independent scalar inputs

A first notation might tempt us toward:

\[
R^* = f(J,W,C,L,F,\ldots)
\]

where justice, warrant, correctness, legitimacy, fittingness, and other dimensions become inputs to a rightness function.

That is probably too simple.

The dimensions appear coupled.

Correctness can alter warrant.

Power can alter legitimacy.

Context can alter fittingness.

Available evidence can alter justification.

Justice can alter what counts as adequate participation.

Temporal distance can transform consequence.

Agency can change what counts as care.

The value or behavior of one dimension may therefore depend partly upon the state of several others.

A better provisional representation is an evolving normative state:

\[
\mathbf{x}(t)
=
(J,W,C,L,F,\ldots)
\]

whose motion depends upon its current state, relational constraints, attention, history, perturbation, observer position, and slice:

\[
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}
=
\mathcal{D}
(\mathbf{x},
\mathcal{C},
A,
H,
P,
s,\ldots)
\]

This is only a gesture toward a mathematical family, not a proposed formalization.

The important claim is that the components are likely **differentially coupled rather than independently summed**.

The existing primitive-basis notation is compatible with leaving the geometry open. Its resolution notes explicitly allow terms whose facets are so entangled that none stands alone, and it refuses to assume a fixed geometry between the decomposed facets and recomposed essence. 

### 9. The geometric object remains deliberately unresolved

Several mathematical pictures are presently plausible.

A **coupled dynamical system** captures mutual perturbation and temporal evolution.

A **phase space** captures trajectories, attractors, metastability, and regime transitions.

A **manifold-like space** captures the possibility that locally useful coordinate systems exist without a globally exhaustive coordinate system.

A **stratified structure** may become necessary if biological, cognitive, interpersonal, institutional, and cultural normative dynamics operate in qualitatively different but connected regimes.

A **bundle-like or gluing structure** may eventually prove useful if different observers possess legitimate local evaluations attached to the same configuration and the global problem concerns whether those local descriptions can be reconciled without deleting their differences.

None should yet be identified as *the* geometry of rightness.

The framework currently warrants saying that the geometry appears **non-scalar, coupled, observer-indexed, temporally dynamic, and probably path-dependent**.

Anything stronger should wait until the formal object earns its name rather than being adopted because topology has particularly seductive vocabulary.

### 10. Closure thresholds need not be uniform

Each evaluative dimension may possess its own thresholds.

A system may cross a threshold for *factually credible* before it crosses one for *justified*.

It may reach *legally permissible* without reaching *morally acceptable*.

It may become *understandable* without becoming *proper*.

This suggests that a higher-order rightness verdict might require some pattern of threshold satisfaction across several coupled dimensions.

Crucially, this should not be understood as averaging scores.

Certain dimensions may impose floors.

Certain failures may be disqualifying.

Certain combinations may transform the relevance of other dimensions.

Some configurations may remain genuinely incomparable.

The system therefore may generate a **partial ordering** rather than a total ranking.

It may be structurally possible to say:

> B is worse than A across every relevant declared dimension.

while remaining unable to derive:

> A must be chosen over C,

when A and C instantiate different legitimate goods and neither dominates the other.

This may explain why E² appears particularly strong at detecting bad closures, hidden burden, overclaimed resolution, excluded participation, domination, and remainder, while being less able to mechanically rank every admissible good configuration.

That may not be a deficiency.

It may be the correct shape of an embedded normative framework.

### 11. Normative emergence, adjudication, warrant, and legitimacy remain distinct

Although they now fit within one dynamical picture, four questions should not be collapsed.

**Normative emergence** asks how preferable and non-preferable distinctions arise.

**Functional adjudication** asks how systems actually close conflicts.

**Normative warrant** asks whether available grounds license a particular closure.

**Procedural legitimacy** asks whether the authority, participation, method, and conditions under which closure occurs are themselves warranted.

A system can possess norms without good adjudication.

It can adjudicate efficiently without warrant.

It can reach a substantively adequate result through an illegitimate process.

It can use a legitimate process and reach an inadequate result.

The dimensions interact, but none substitutes for the others.

### 12. Rightness may be recursive without being viciously circular

Any verdict can itself become the configuration presented to another evaluative layer.

This means adjudication can adjudicate adjudication.

Warrant can itself be warranted or challenged.

Procedural legitimacy can itself be evaluated.

At first this looks like an infinite regress.

But E² need not eliminate the regress by pretending to possess a final self-certifying standpoint.

Instead, closure can occur provisionally under declared dependence, finite resolution, and maintained remainder.

The question becomes not:

> Can this judgment prove itself from nowhere?

but:

> Does this closure possess sufficient warrant for the action and scope it claims, and does it remain appropriately reopenable?

This is close to the essay's own conclusion that a chain of reasoning may function derivationally while eventually reaching commitments that must be held, negotiated, contested, and inherited rather than independently proven. 

### 13. The relationship between right and good remains open

*Good* should not yet simply be added as one more coordinate.

There are reasons to suspect that goodness and rightness are deeply coupled but not equivalent.

Goodness may concern something like positive valence, benefit, flourishing, desirable states, or attractor quality.

Rightness may concern something more like appropriate alignment, selection, fit, admissibility, or justified closure among competing possibilities.

A thing can be good without being right in a particular configuration.

Rest may be good while resting during an emergency is not right.

Protection may be good while protection that destroys another's agency may not remain right.

Truth may be good while every conceivable use of every truth is not therefore right.

Conversely, judgments of what is right shape what systems learn to regard as good.

The current relationship may therefore be better represented provisionally as coupling:

\[
Good \leftrightarrow Right
\]

rather than reduction in either direction.

The essay already contains this unresolved relation, moving from persistence and preference toward “good” while treating rightness as the more complex relational formation that governs and coordinates competing states. 

### 14. `right*` is a resolution convention, not a metaphysical object

Under the Primitive-Basis Resolution convention, `right*` should mean **right taken under its current resolved closure**, with its complexity preserved in an associated resolution note.

It does not denote absolute Rightness, an ultimate object, or a fully accessible global state.

A resolution remains slice-bound, provisional, and explicitly carries remainder. The notation itself states that a starred term is a representation invoked rather than a truth asserted.  

Thus:

\[
right^*[s]
\]

can be used to mean the present resolved understanding of rightness under slice \(s\), without claiming that the slice exhausts the referent.

This is especially appropriate here because the object under inquiry appears precisely to resist a final, frame-free definition.

### 15. Provisional working statement

A compact form of the proposal might therefore be:

> **Rightness is a distributed, metastable relational configuration arising among embedded systems for whom possible states are non-equivalent. It is encountered through observer-indexed slices and expressed through partially independent but dynamically coupled forms of evaluation such as correctness, warrant, justice, legitimacy, propriety, fittingness, and ethical adequacy. These do not combine as independent scalar inputs to a single score. They evolve under mutual constraint, history, attention, power, consequence, and changing resolution.**
>
> **Normative closure occurs when an evolving evaluative configuration crosses a context-dependent threshold and a system commits to a state, action, or classification. The resulting verdict is distinct from both the closure that produced it and the adequacy of that closure relative to the wider field. Verdicts may themselves become objects of higher-order adjudication, allowing biological, cognitive, social, and institutional systems to affirm, revise, qualify, or reopen previous closure.**
>
> **Warrant is local and indexed: it concerns whether the relational grounds available to an observer at a given slice sufficiently license a closure. A locally warranted verdict need not remain warranted when transported into a wider frame. Rightness therefore cannot be established merely by successful closure, stability, consensus, causal explanation, or local coherence.**
>
> **At larger scales, relevant information and consequences are distributed among differently situated observers. A more resolved rightness judgment consequently requires increasing attention to participation, localization, cross-domain coupling, power, temporal effects, contestability, and remainder. Some normative floors and dominated configurations may be structurally identifiable, while selection among multiple non-dominated configurations may remain underdetermined and require legitimate relational commitment rather than derivation from a universal scalar.**
>
> **The mathematical character of this structure remains open. Its current behavior is more consistent with a coupled, nonlinear, observer-indexed, path-dependent dynamical field or state space than with a simple function. The relationship between rightness and goodness remains unresolved and may itself be one of dynamic coupling rather than inclusion or identity. `right*` names only the current resolved representation of this complex referent under a declared slice, never its final or complete determination.**

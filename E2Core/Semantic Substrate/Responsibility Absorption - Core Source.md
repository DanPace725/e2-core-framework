## 1. Candidate Claim

Responsibility absorption is a routing move in which a locus voluntarily accepts a primary obligation to answer, investigate, repair, and prevent recurrence for a distributed failure.

Absorption does not assert sole causation, erase other duties, or convert the absorbing locus into the only valid narrator. It changes the repair route while preserving the causal and consequence ledgers.

The move is coherent when consolidation makes repair more reachable without concealing displaced consequence or foreclosing contestability.

## 2. Distinctions

### Causal attribution

Who and what contributed to the failure. This ledger may remain distributed across people, systems, incentives, vendors, interfaces, and background conditions.

### Internal blameless review

A diagnostic practice that reduces individual defensive pressure to improve mechanism discovery. It concerns how an organization investigates internally. It does not decide who owes repair externally.

### Responsibility absorption

Acceptance of a primary repair and answerability route by a locus with relevant capacity. It concerns where affected parties can turn and who commits resources.

### Liability and sanction

Legal or institutional assignment under an applicable regime. It may overlap with absorption but is not defined by it.

### Moral exoneration

A judgment about blame or fault. Absorption neither requires nor entails exoneration of other participants.

Keeping these objects separate allows an organization to say `we will repair this` without saying `only we caused this` or `no one else has duties`.

## 3. Admissibility Conditions

Responsibility absorption is provisionally admissible only when the following conditions hold.

1. **Repair capacity:** the locus controls or can coordinate material levers relevant to investigation, containment, remedy, and recurrence prevention.
2. **Reachability:** affected parties can identify and contact the locus through a durable channel.
3. **Ledger preservation:** causal contributors, external consequences, uncertainty, and remainder remain recorded rather than being collapsed into the absorbing identity.
4. **Contestability:** affected parties and independent reviewers can challenge the account, evidence, remedy, and declared completion.
5. **Non-displacement:** absorption does not move unrepaired cost onto parties outside the absorbing boundary.
6. **Proportional scope:** the locus states which obligations it accepts, which it shares, and which remain outside its control.
7. **Temporal commitment:** the route persists long enough for repair, follow-up, and recurrence checks, with a successor if the locus changes.

Absorption that lacks repair capacity is symbolic capture: it terminates search without making repair more likely. Absorption that lacks contestability concentrates narrative power alongside repair power.

## 4. Multi-Locus Ledger

Responsibility absorption does not require a single-entry ledger.

For an incident `e`, let each reachable locus `Li` carry a typed obligation vector:

```text
Ri(e) = <answer, investigate, contain, repair, compensate,
         disclose, prevent, preserve evidence, support contest>
```

Absorption means one locus accepts primary coordination for a declared subset of that vector. Other loci may retain duties based on causal contribution, authority, benefit, custody, contract, or consequence-bearing.

No conservation law is asserted over blame. The invariant is procedural: every material consequence and required repair must remain routed or explicitly unresolved.

## 5. Narrative Concentration

The party that absorbs responsibility often gains privileged control over records, timing, terminology, and public explanation. That can improve coherence and speed while also narrowing what the incident is allowed to mean.

Counterweights include:

- independent evidence preservation;
- affected-party accounts attached to the record;
- external review rights;
- disclosure of uncertainty and disagreement;
- remedy criteria defined before the absorbing party declares completion;
- appeal or escalation paths not controlled solely by the absorber.

Narrative plurality is not an obstacle to repair. It is part of the contestability architecture that keeps absorption from becoming laundering.

## 6. Relationship To Consequence Routing

Consequence Routing asks where typed pressure should move toward answerability, repair, constraint, custody, or remainder-bearing. Responsibility absorption is one routing strategy when distributed ownership would otherwise leave the aggregate failure owned by nobody.

The strategy should be preferred only when it improves:

- route clarity;
- repair capacity;
- temporal availability;
- evidence preservation;
- affected-party access;
- recurrence prevention.

It should be rejected or narrowed when it reduces:

- causal resolution needed for repair;
- independent attribution channels;
- affected-party standing;
- contestability;
- access to compensation or remedy;
- visibility of consequences outside the absorbing boundary.

## 7. Failure Modes

- **Heroic absorption:** accepting responsibility beyond actual control or capacity.
- **Narrative capture:** using ownership of repair to monopolize interpretation.
- **Blamelessness export:** maintaining internal psychological safety while denying external consequence bearers a route.
- **Duty erasure:** treating primary coordination as cancellation of other loci's obligations.
- **Premature closure:** declaring the incident resolved when remainder persists.
- **Scapegoat absorption:** concentrating responsibility in a replaceable individual who lacks structural control.
- **Insurance without learning:** compensating visible losses while leaving the generating mechanism intact.
- **Endless ownership:** retaining the role after repair in a way that prevents distributed capability or successor accountability.

## 8. Minimum Trace

An absorption record should state:

- absorbing locus and legitimate authority;
- obligations accepted;
- material levers controlled;
- other loci and duties preserved;
- affected parties and consequence channels;
- evidence custody and independent access;
- remedy and completion criteria;
- contest and escalation routes;
- unresolved remainder;
- review date and successor arrangement.

## Final Compression

Responsibility absorption consolidates a repair route without consolidating reality. It is coherent only when the absorber is reachable and capable, the causal and consequence ledgers stay open, affected parties retain contest power, and unrepaired remainder is not pushed outside the boundary.

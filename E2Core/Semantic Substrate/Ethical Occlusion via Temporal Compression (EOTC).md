# Ethical Occlusion via Temporal Compression (EOTC) {#title}

EOTC states that temporal compression can create epistemic and ethical singularities: zones where causality, responsibility, and observability decouple because the system is moving faster than its resolution capacity.

Ethical failure here is often geometry before malice. A system can become too fast, too layered, or too delayed for its accountability mechanisms to see what it is doing.

## Core Model {#core-model}

For a polytemporal system with natural tempo `T_i` and imposed tempo `T_imp_i`:

```text
C_i = T_i / T_imp_i
```

`C_i` is layer compression. Effective compression `C_eff` depends on the coupling and dominance of compressed layers.

As compression rises:

- consequence-distance increases;
- observability decays;
- attribution closure becomes harder;
- ethical remainder accumulates;
- systems drift toward accountability theater unless decompressed or redesigned.

## Resolution-Responsibility Design Axiom {#resolution-responsibility}

```text
R * C_eff <= R_max
```

where:

- `R` is the resolution required to distinguish relevant causal/ethical structure;
- `C_eff` is effective temporal compression;
- `R_max` is the available observation/response capacity.

If the product of resolution demand and compression exceeds capacity, the system cannot maintain fine-grained responsibility honestly. It must slow down, coarsen grain, expand capacity, or name unresolved remainder.

## Occlusion Sources {#occlusion-sources}

Ethical occlusion rises from:

- speed beyond observation;
- delayed consequences;
- low-resolution instrumentation;
- fragmented decision chains;
- category collapse;
- incentive pressure toward fast closure;
- adversarial use of fog, laundering, or temporal arbitrage.

## Observability {#observability}

Observability is not binary. It is a probabilistic and relational property. A system can see some consequences, at some scale, with some delay, from some observer position.

Design should declare:

- what can be observed;
- what cannot be observed;
- what is inferred;
- what is below resolution;
- what is being carried as remainder.

## Coherence and Bookkeeping {#coherence}

Compression does not eliminate ethical mass. It displaces it. Harm, responsibility, and consequence either land in accountable structures or persist as unresolved remainder.

EOTC therefore requires bookkeeping:

- track what was decided too fast to resolve;
- track who benefits from unresolved speed;
- track which harms appear only after delay;
- track which decisions were below the system's resolution floor.

## Operating Zones {#zones}

- **Zone 1:** low compression; direct feedback; direct repair often possible.
- **Zone 2:** managed compression; systems stewardship and instrumentation matter.
- **Zone 3:** high complexity; prediction becomes participation; design for resilience.
- **Zone 4:** hypothetical or extreme threshold; old accountability categories fail.

## Design Principles {#design-principles}

- Slow down high-stakes decisions when resolution is inadequate.
- Match attribution grain to scale.
- Preserve audit trails across time.
- Increase observability before increasing speed.
- Create decompression intervals for metabolization.
- Treat remainder as a named object, not a disposal channel.
- Resist category precompression: do not call complex causal fields "simple accountability" just to close faster.

## Worked Examples {#worked-examples}

- **Driving:** speed compresses reaction time; ethical responsibility depends on visibility, latency, and capacity.
- **Factories:** throughput pressure can hide injury, fatigue, and maintenance debt until after harm crystallizes.
- **Finance:** high-speed abstractions can move risk faster than accountability can follow.
- **Software deployment:** rapid release cycles can create invisible downstream harms unless observability and rollback capacity scale with speed.
- **Institutional decisions:** committee fragmentation can spread agency below attribution thresholds.

## Glossary {#glossary}

**Temporal compression:** imposed reduction in the time available for a process relative to its natural or safe tempo.

**Effective compression (`C_eff`):** the operative compression felt by a coupled system after layers interact.

**Ethical occlusion:** loss of visibility into consequence, responsibility, or harm due to compression, complexity, delay, or low resolution.

**Resolution (`R`):** the granularity required to distinguish morally or causally relevant differences.

**Resolution capacity (`R_max`):** the maximum observation/response capacity available to a system.

**Consequence-distance:** separation between action and observable effect across time, space, mediation, or abstraction.

**Remainder:** unresolved ethical or causal mass that cannot honestly be assigned at current resolution.

**Decompression:** slowing, spacing, staging, or otherwise increasing the time and attention available for observation and response.

**Temporal arbitrage:** exploiting monitoring gaps, delays, or speed mismatches to avoid accountability.

**Accountability theater:** performance of responsibility in conditions where the system lacks the resolution to actually assign or discharge it.

## Limitations {#limitations}

EOTC does not claim that all ethical failure is caused by speed. It claims that speed and compression create predictable conditions under which ethical perception and attribution fail. Malice, negligence, and extraction can exploit those conditions, but they are not required for occlusion to emerge.

## Summary {#summary}

Ethical occlusion via temporal compression names the point at which speed outruns responsibility. The remedy is not simply more virtue. It is better temporal design: slower critical paths, clearer resolution floors, stronger observability, honest remainder tracking, and accountability mechanisms matched to scale.

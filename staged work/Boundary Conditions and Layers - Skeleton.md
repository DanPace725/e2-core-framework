# Boundary Conditions and Layers - Skeleton

Status: staged skeleton

Purpose: gather the general patterns around boundary layering, contact conditions, interface films, transition thresholds, and substrate formation without mixing them into either `Boundary Dynamics` or the measurement protocol.

Proposed role:

```text
Boundary Dynamics = structural/operator theory.
Boundary and Horizon = epistemic/access theory.
Boundary Conditions and Layers = stratification, contact conditions, interface formation, and transition thresholds.
```

Current boundary material mixes:

## 5. Local Interface Films

Working concept:

Local interface films are fast contact-layer phenomena generated when bounded agents or systems enter proximity.

Candidate properties:

- boundary-mediated,
- generated by interaction,
- shaped by self-field/boundary state,
- fragile under low repetition,
- capable of stabilizing through memory and repeated contact.

Questions:

- Is "film" the right term, or should this be "interface layer"?
- How literal should the boundary-layer analogy be?
- Does this belong here or in `Interface Substrate Ontology`?

## 6. Bound Collective Substrate

Working concept:

Bound collective substrate appears when local interface effects become self-maintaining across agent turnover and begin constraining future interactions as a field property.

Candidate compact form:

```text
local film becomes bound substrate when the relation no longer depends entirely
on the initiating dyads to preserve its contact conditions.
```

Candidate threshold function:

```text
theta_bind = f(repetition, memory, cross-dyadic transfer, normative inertia, load transfer)
```

Questions:

- Is this a boundary-layer document or an interface-substrate document?
- What evidence indicates substrate has become self-conditioning?
- How much formalism is useful at this stage?

## 7. Transition Conditions

Candidate threshold conditions:

### 7.1 Repetition

Contacts recur often enough that the interface is not reset to zero.

### 7.2 Memory

The field preserves traces of prior contact: signs, rituals, precedents, repair pathways, shared references, embodied expectation.

### 7.3 Cross-Dyadic Transfer

Stability generated in one relation becomes available to other participants.

### 7.4 Normative Inertia

Newcomers encounter "how things are done here" as a condition of the field.

### 7.5 Load Transfer

The layer carries friction that agents previously had to carry personally.

Question:

Are these five enough, too many, or the wrong type of list?

## 8. Pluralistic Boundary Conditions

Working claim:

Healthy pluralism is neither merger nor separation. It requires interface-maintenance structures that preserve difference while allowing metabolizable contact.

# Boundary and Horizon - Skeleton

Status: staged skeleton

Purpose: separate the epistemic/access layer of boundary work from the structural/operator layer already handled by `Boundary Dynamics`.

Proposed role:

```text
Boundary Dynamics = what boundaries are and how they operate.
Boundary and Horizon = what boundaries make accessible, inaccessible, visible, occluded, or redshifted from an observer position.
```

## 1. Core Distinction

Working distinction:

```text
Boundary = structural differentiation that permits form and regulates engagement.
Horizon  = access-limit produced by that form from a given observer position.
```

Questions to settle:

- Is horizon a construct, relation, observer-position property, or epistemic operator?
- Does every boundary generate horizons, or only boundaries relative to observers?
- What language avoids implying a new relational primitive?

## 2. Relation To Boundary Dynamics

Boundary Dynamics owns:

- boundary as relational operator,
- boundary modes,
- mode-capacity,
- selectivity,
- conservation,
- thresholds,
- failure modes.

Boundary and Horizon should own:

- access limits,
- observer position,
- epistemic finitude,
- remainder beyond access,
- occlusion as horizon-totalization,
- redshifted remainder.

Candidate cross-link:

```text
Boundary Dynamics describes the structural/operator layer.
Boundary and Horizon describes the epistemic/access layer generated by boundary-mediated form.
```

## 3. Resolution, Boundary, Horizon, Remainder

Candidate compact form:

```text
resolution -> boundary + horizon + remainder
```

Working claims:

- Resolution stabilizes coherence by accepting loss.
- Boundary is the cut/operator that permits form.
- Horizon is the access-limit created by that form.
- Remainder is what exceeds present access.

Needed refinement:

- How does this connect to MMPS without duplicating MMPS?
- Is remainder always beyond horizon, or can remainder also exist inside a boundary as unresolved content?

## 4. Occlusion

Candidate definition:

```text
Occlusion = horizon-totalization.
```

Meaning:

The observer mistakes the limit of present access for the limit of what exists.

Questions:

- How does this relate to existing P5 occlusion language?
- How does this relate to AOMI's adversarial occlusion?
- What differentiates ordinary limitation from harmful occlusion?

## 5. Redshifted Remainder

Candidate definition:

```text
Redshifted remainder = real signal beyond the current horizon that remains present
but is attenuated below recognition by observer position, bias-field curvature,
compression, noise, or receiver tuning.
```

Likely parent:

- Signal as Bias Field
- AOMI

Guardrail:

Treat redshift as candidate-formal until the attenuation relation is specified.

Questions:

- Does redshift belong here, or mainly in a Signal as Bias Field addendum?
- What minimal formal expression is useful without overclaiming?
- How does redshift differ from noise, absence, disagreement, or ignorance?

## 6. Horizon Patterns

Possible patterns:

- healthy boundary, acknowledged horizon,
- healthy boundary, totalized horizon,
- failed boundary, unstable horizon,
- captured boundary, constructed horizon,
- over-hardened boundary, shrinking horizon.

Questions:

- Which patterns are general enough to include?
- Which belong in measurement/evaluation instead?

## 7. Adversarial Horizon Construction

Working claim:

Adversarial occlusion can be read as deliberate horizon construction plus horizon-totalization.

Possible mechanisms:

- move resolution center,
- increase noise,
- raise recognition threshold,
- force premature closure,
- overload bandwidth,
- destroy intermediate signs,
- make real signal appear irrelevant, dangerous, ridiculous, or absent.

Questions:

- Should this be a short subsection here and a fuller AOMI extension later?
- How much operational detail belongs in the general source?

Candidate principle list:

- redshift makes presence unreadable,
- position matters,
- resolution matters,

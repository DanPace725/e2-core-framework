# PLTi Attention Addendum — Developmental Reading

*September 28, 2026 · staged source reading, not a promotion review*

**Inputs:** the unchanged [Attention Node Addendum](Attention%20Node%20Addendum%20PLTi%20Refinements.md), [Kothari et al. (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-026-72340-9), and the [AAF candidate](../20260924/Attentional%20Access%20and%20Formation%20-%20Candidate%20Source.md). This note records how the addendum informed AAF v0.3. The paper reports a mouse spatial-attention result; the Gate/Ward, Pick, Lean, GCO, and CFAR readings are corpus interpretations.

The useful empirical separation is bounded but clear. Bilateral PLTi silencing impaired target selection with competing task-relevant flankers. The tested single-target discrimination, upper/lower port-choice rates, and orienting trajectories did not show corresponding impairment. With matched-salience but task-irrelevant weak flankers, a small control group showed no discernible disruption. The paper also found a shifted and less categorical behavioral selection boundary and a corresponding pattern in superior-colliculus recordings under a separate passive-mouse competition protocol. These results make *selection-boundary position* and *sharpness* good questions for AAF's Pick, alongside its prior aperture and multiplicity language. They support distinguishing competitive selection from the tested perceptual and motor measures at that experimental resolution.

AAF v0.3 therefore adds a worked neural case, the position/sharpness distinction, and a contrast between broad Alert changes and selective Ward failure as a **future discriminating experiment**. It does not assign PLTi definitively to the `G` term of the earlier priority sketch. The paper's task-relevance result shows that salience alone is insufficient for the observed deficit; it does not locate where relevance and salience combine, nor prove that PLTi merely gates an already-combined priority. A shifted boundary likewise does not identify inherited Lean without a history or intervention on terrain.

The addendum's “Ward makes Pick” proposal remains creatively useful. Broad competitive inhibition in birds and the mammalian behavioral/neural result suggest a possible way a graded field could yield categorical selection. The mammalian PLTi topology and compute-versus-relay question remain open in the paper. The proposed decomposition of CFAR's `k₁` into perceptual and competitive-selection terms is an analogy for later modeling, not a fitted parameter interpretation or a warranted active CFAR edit. Faster reaction times and a lower drift-diffusion threshold are reported separately from the task-specific accuracy deficit; equating that fitted threshold with GCO or AMM's “firing early” is a further corpus hypothesis.

This is a useful convergence with an existing separation in AAF, not independent confirmation of AAF's whole cross-substrate architecture or a novel discovery of standard selective-attention mechanics. The main silencing sample was small (n=6), the task-irrelevant control smaller (n=3), and analyses were not blinded. The next creative step would be an explicit comparison of relevant and irrelevant competitors under independently varied arousal and inhibitory control, keeping sensory discrimination, selection-boundary shape, and response threshold as distinct observations. No clinical phenotype follows from this result alone.

**Status:** original addendum preserved unchanged in staged work; bounded empirical distinctions incorporated into AAF v0.3; proposed circuit placement and CFAR/AMM mappings remain staged leads.

## Core Claim

Boundary and horizon are coupled but distinct.

```text
Boundary = structural differentiation that permits form and regulates engagement.
Horizon  = access-limit produced by that form from a given observer position.
```

Boundary belongs to the structural/operator layer. It explains how regions become distinct, engage, refuse, transduce, route, exchange, and conserve.

Horizon belongs to the epistemic/access layer. It explains how a boundary produces limits of perception, causality, interpretation, recognition, and action for an observer.

Every boundary can generate horizons. Not every horizon is itself a boundary.

## Relation To Boundary Dynamics

Boundary Dynamics remains the primary structural account.

- relational, not edge-like,
- active, not passive,
- observer-relative,

It adds the epistemic companion:

```text
If Boundary Dynamics asks, "What does the boundary do?"
Boundary and Horizon asks, "What does the boundary make accessible or inaccessible from here?"
```

## Boundary

A boundary is a structural operator in relation-space.

It is not a static line between already-finished things. It is the typed structure of engagement between regions.

A boundary:

- differentiates,
- regulates contact,
- admits and refuses,
- transduces and reflects,
- hosts closure events,
- carries mode-capacity,
- has selectivity,
- changes under load,
- appears differently at different resolutions.

Boundary gives form.

Without boundary, no coherent object, region, identity, category, institution, relationship, practice, or field can hold its distinction long enough to operate.

## Horizon

A horizon is the access-limit produced by form.

It is observer-relative. It says:

```text
beyond this, something continues,
but not in a way presently accessible from here.
```

Horizon is not mere ignorance. It is structured finitude. It discloses the limits produced by position, resolution, boundary geometry, bias-field curvature, temporal frame, and available instruments.

A horizon may limit:

- perception,
- interpretation,
- recognition,
- causal influence,
- moral imagination,
- operational reach,
- memory,
- prediction,
- responsibility,
- repair.

Horizon gives positional finitude.

## Resolution Produces Boundary And Horizon

Resolution stabilizes coherence by accepting loss.

Every act of resolution draws a form. Drawing a form produces a boundary. Producing a boundary also produces a horizon: an outside, remainder, or beyond that cannot be fully accessed from inside the resolved form.

Compact form:

```text
resolution -> boundary + horizon + remainder
```

The boundary is the cut that permits coherence.

The horizon is the access-limit created by that cut.

The remainder is what exceeds current access.

## The Five-Term System

| Term | Function |
| --- | --- |
| resolution | accepts loss to stabilize coherence |
| boundary | cut/operator that permits form |
| horizon | access-limit produced by form |
| remainder | what exceeds present access |
| occlusion | mistaking the horizon for the whole |

This system lets the framework distinguish structural difference from epistemic limitation.

Boundary says:

```text
this is not that
```

Horizon says:

```text
from here, access runs out
```

Remainder says:

```text
what exceeds access has not thereby ceased to exist
```

Occlusion says:

```text
the observer has mistaken access-limit for reality-limit
```

## Occlusion As Horizon-Totalization

Occlusion is not simply ignorance, denial, or error.

Occlusion is horizon-totalization: treating the limit of present access as the limit of what exists.

This distinction matters because an observer can be sincere and still occluded. The observer may not be refusing visible evidence. The evidence may be beyond the observer's current horizon, or present only in a form the observer cannot recognize.

In this sense, occlusion is often a failure of horizon humility.

The observer says, implicitly:

```text
I cannot access it from here.
Therefore it is not real, not relevant, or not signal.
```

The correction is not always more information. Sometimes the correction is a change in position, resolution, tempo, instrument, relation, or receiver tuning.

## Redshifted Remainder

Some remainder is not absent. It is present but attenuated below recognition.

The staged materials name this **redshifted remainder**.

Candidate definition:

```text
Redshifted remainder is signal beyond the current horizon that remains present
in the field but is attenuated below recognition by observer position,
bias-field curvature, compression, noise, or receiver tuning.
```

The concept belongs near Signal as Bias Field, but it is useful here because it explains how horizons become experientially total.

The observer does not simply lack the message. The signal has become unreadable as signal.

Practical consequence:

```text
If redshift is the mechanism, louder transmission is usually the wrong intervention.
```

Possible interventions include:

- receiver retuning,
- bias-field reduction,
- change of observer position,
- slower tempo,
- intermediate signs,
- contact zones,
- horizon reconfiguration.

## Adversarial Horizon Construction

AOMI can be sharpened through horizon language.

Adversarial occlusion is deliberate horizon construction plus horizon-totalization.

An adversarial system does not only hide information. It can alter the target's recognition conditions so that information no longer counts as information.

It may:

- move the target's resolution center,
- increase noise,
- raise recognition thresholds,
- force premature closure,
- overload bandwidth,
- degrade receiver trust,
- destroy intermediate signs,
- make real signals appear irrelevant, dangerous, ridiculous, or absent.

The target then totalizes the constructed horizon:

```text
what I can see is all there is
```

The attack succeeds when the horizon is mistaken for the world.

## Boundary/Horizon Coupling Patterns

### Healthy Boundary, Acknowledged Horizon

The boundary permits coherent form, and the observer recognizes that the form generates limits.

Pattern:

```text
coherence + humility
```

### Healthy Boundary, Totalized Horizon

The boundary works structurally, but the observer mistakes its horizon for reality.

Pattern:

```text
coherence + occlusion
```

### Failed Boundary, Unstable Horizon

The boundary cannot preserve coherent distinction. The observer's access-limit shifts chaotically because the form itself does not hold.

Pattern:

```text
fragmentation + disorientation
```

### Captured Boundary, Constructed Horizon

The boundary's selectivity is source-distorted. The horizon is shaped by power, capture, or adversarial design.

Pattern:

```text
controlled access + false world
```

### Over-Hardened Boundary, Shrinking Horizon

The boundary preserves distinction by refusing too much contact. The horizon contracts, and remainder is recoded as threat or contamination.

Pattern:

```text
rigidity + narrowing
```

## Layers And Conditions

Boundary Dynamics already contains nesting, layering, and coupling.

This artifact adds one general principle:

```text
as boundaries become more complex, they generate layered horizons.
```

A layered boundary may produce different horizons at different layers:

- material horizon,
- operational horizon,
- informational horizon,
- social horizon,
- symbolic horizon,
- moral horizon,
- temporal horizon.

For example, an institution may have:

- a formal legal boundary,
- an informal social boundary,
- an epistemic boundary around what can be known,
- a moral boundary around what can be acknowledged,
- and a temporal boundary around what history is allowed to matter.

Each layer may admit different signals and redshift different remainder.

This should eventually connect to a separate `Boundary Conditions and Layers` artifact. The present document only establishes the conceptual relation.

## General Principles

### 1. Boundaries Generate Horizons

Every stable form creates an access-limit relative to observers.

### 2. Horizons Are Not Nothing

A horizon is a structured limit, not absence. Something may continue beyond it.

### 3. Remainder Persists

What exceeds current access remains part of the field's unfinished accounting.

### 4. Occlusion Totalizes Horizon

Occlusion occurs when the observer treats access-limit as reality-limit.

### 5. Redshift Makes Presence Unreadable

Some signals are present but attenuated below recognition.

### 6. Position Matters

Changing observer position can change the horizon without changing the underlying field.

### 7. Resolution Matters

Changing resolution can make a boundary appear as edge, gradient, process, coherence window, or artifact.

### 8. Time Matters

Some horizons only become visible across temporal frames. Recovery, decay, repetition, and memory are often invisible in moment-scale observation.

### 9. Power Matters

Captured boundaries and adversarial systems can construct horizons for others.

### 10. Humility Is Structural

Horizon-awareness is not merely a virtue. It is an epistemic requirement for valid boundary interpretation.

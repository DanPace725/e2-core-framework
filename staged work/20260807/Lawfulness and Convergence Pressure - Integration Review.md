## Collision And Risk Review

- Naming collision: "lawfulness" carries legal, moral, and philosophy-of-science meanings absent from the local corpus search but unavoidable outside it.
- Register collision: treating lawfulness as a permissibility condition may confuse epistemic license for probability with ethical permission.
- Formal gap: "stable measure" has no selected definition, test, or invariance class.
- Regime overclaim: the four regimes may be useful distinctions, but exclusivity and exhaustiveness are not derived.
- Scale ambiguity: event-scale and ensemble-scale claims require an explicit coarse-graining map and observer resolution.
- Relevance gap: without a relevant/irrelevant observable criterion, convergence pressure remains descriptive.
- Reflexivity risk: Lucas critique, Goodhart effects, evaluation awareness, and strategic adaptation may share geometry without being one mechanism.
- Empirical-source risk: the AI observations require per-claim primary citations and do not by themselves validate the formal object.

## Proposed Integration Method

Promote only the formal kernel into a candidate note:

- define lawfulness with observer, scale, window, and perturbation class as explicit arguments;
- compare stationarity, structural stability, and invariance-class definitions;
- give counterexamples separating possible-but-lawless from merely unknown;
- derive the relationship to EDF conditioning and TCL lamination;
- treat Convergence Pressure as an alias or ensemble-scale corollary until it predicts relevance;
- retain Reflexive Attenuation as a provisional failure label;
- leave Coherence Amplification Ratio and Slice Epistemics as future work.

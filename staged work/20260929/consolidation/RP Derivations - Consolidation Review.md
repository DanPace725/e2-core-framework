## Executive reading

The active `Relational Primitives` ORMD is the best current home for the six-part relational grammar. It integrates the earlier compact formal source and the V3 physics-grounded derivation, and its categorical section now calls the mapping a translation/analogue rather than proof of equivalence. It also limits the primitives to working categories, not metaphysical absolutes, and says resemblance across domains needs a specified mapping and evidence.

The three other active pairs are not equivalent derivations of that grammar:

- `Derivation deep dive` is a conversational survey and large proposed relationship inventory across matter, energy, and spacetime. Some of its useful history and categories informed the V3 derivation, but its exhaustive-sounding coverage claims and many physics mappings are not demonstrated.
- `CT translation of RPs` proposes a categorical encoding for each primitive. It contains useful analogies but elevates them into a one-to-one formalization/completeness claim without defining enough structure to establish it.
- `RP Lambda Calc Translation` attempts to encode the E² “ladder” as lambda terms. The displayed definitions do not model the claimed primitive stages or closure behavior; several conclusions do not follow from lambda calculus notation.

The proposed “one coherent derivation document” should therefore be a scoped, reviewed candidate derived from the active RP source and these source histories, not a concatenation or synthesis that inherits their strongest claims. The three pairs are **not yet ready for archival**: an explicit destination and retention record are needed, and the CT/Lambda references and lineage identifiers need repair first.

## Unsupported equivalences and category errors to prevent

### Category Theory

- **Analogy is not equivalence.** Assigning each RP a categorical analogue does not give a bijection between six primitives and six “fundamental” categorical structures, nor prove the primitives complete. Categories have objects and morphisms by definition, but a given physical interpretation must still specify what those are.
- **Monoidal composition does not automatically identify physical composition.** A tensor product can model a composite system in a chosen category, but `A ⊗ B ⊗ C` alone does not derive a proton or its binding dynamics.
- **The terminal-object equation in the CT draft is not a no-signaling test as written.** If `1` is terminal, the morphisms `top_B ∘ f` and `top_A` are equal for any `f:A→B` simply by uniqueness of the map to the terminal object. Additional structure is needed to express a physical causal/no-signaling condition.
- **Equalizers model only constraints represented by suitable parallel arrows.** A formal equalizer may be useful for a particular constraint model; it does not imply all physical laws or constraints are limits.
- **Kleisli categories are optional modeling machinery.** A probability monad can encode stochastic channels in a specified setting. Measurement and epistemic access are not inherently a Kleisli category, and entropy is not defined merely by changing category.
- **Functors/adjunctions do not exhaust meta-relations.** They are precise structures for mappings between categories when their data and laws are supplied, not proof that every cross-theory relation takes that form.

### Lambda Calculus

- The “ladder” levels are not distinct under the source's definitions: with `P` the identity and `C(f)=f∘f`, applying `C` to `P` returns `P`.
- A fixed-point combinator is a formal recursion device. Its presence does not guarantee a terminating evaluation, a convergent sequence, a stable physical state, or a “fixed point of existence.”
- The nested abstraction `λm.λg.λk.λt.P` is a function awaiting arguments; as displayed, it does not apply persistence, crystallization, geometry, or ethics to `P`.
- The human-facing interpretation “C stabilizes structure” is not encoded by the displayed function-squaring term. A separate definition plus proof would be required.

## Working read

The load-bearing contribution is a proposed problem frame: actors may exploit visibility, attribution, timing, topology, or measurement gaps in accountability mechanisms. The dialogue calls this a possible “adversarial ethics”/“ethical exploit theory”; the active 385-word ORMD rewrites it as AOMI doctrine. The much larger AOMI V1 specification already develops this frame into threat categories, design patterns, metrics, implementation sketches, and a TC/EO companion. The dialogue pair therefore appears superseded as an active conceptual source, while remaining useful as dated provenance and as evidence of which claims entered the later specification.

## Evidence limitations and open checks before archival

- AI responses are idea generation, not independent peer review, literature verification, or empirical evidence. Assistant agreement and confident prose do not establish novelty, correctness, or effectiveness.
- The dialogue does not provide citations for claims about existing disciplines, ethical incompleteness, the 2008 crisis, or computational irreducibility.
- V1 contains many precise percentages, success metrics, SLOs, and roadmap promises (for example 90%/60% targets). No dataset, measurement protocol, operational deployment, or results were found in the reviewed sources; label these as aspirational placeholders unless separately substantiated.
- “Ethical occlusion is geometric, not malicious” is a useful separation between structural conditions and actor intent, but later threat-model language sometimes attributes deliberate strategy. Retain that distinction: occlusion can arise structurally; exploiting it requires evidence of actor behavior/intent.

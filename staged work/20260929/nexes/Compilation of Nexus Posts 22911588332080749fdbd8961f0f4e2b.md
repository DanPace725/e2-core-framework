# **How I Stumbled Into an Updated Framework for Human Connection**

## **The Unexpected Turn**

In 2022, I was working data entry to pay the bills after my photography businesses collapsed during the pandemic. I had ventured into crypto consultancy but the FTX/Luna/Celsius fiasco crashed the market and while I didn’t lose any money, the state of the crypto industry at the time was not looking promising.

Not exactly where I'd imagined I'd be at 30, but life has a way of humbling your plans.

Then ChatGPT launched in November 2022, and I knew immediately that everything was about to change; not just technologically, but psychologically, philosophically, culturally, and ethically. While everyone was debating whether AI would take jobs or write better poetry, I became obsessed with a different question: What happens to human meaning-making when we're suddenly in relationship with systems that can think?

## **Twenty Years of Preparation I Didn't Know I Was Doing**

I've been studying psychology independently since I was 13, reading college textbooks for fun and starting a psych degree at 18. Military service, campus ministry with hundreds of college students, finishing my AS in psychology, then pivoting to entrepreneurship, with a host of other life events in-between, … none of it felt particularly coherent at the time. But I did well in every new environment and saying I “learned alot” seems like a bit of an understatement.

The real plot twist came at 29 when I was diagnosed with ASD and ADHD. Suddenly, decades of feeling like I understood people differently than they understood themselves made sense. My pattern-recognition abilities, my obsession with interdisciplinary connections, my tendency to see systems where others saw isolated events, all of it was now reframed and it changed just about everything related to how I saw myself and the world.

With four kids at home and bills to pay, I threw myself into studying everything I could about the intersection of human psychology and emerging AI. Computer science, complexity theory, quantum physics, systems thinking, ecology. I was trying to understand not just how AI worked, but how it was already changing us. I wanted to understand everything holistically, and develop some way to communicate and translate how I saw the world.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!nGmA!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F9dd077e6-b840-47e7-912b-8ff28015719f_1092x588.png)

## **The Breakthrough That Started Everything**

In July 2023, while synthesizing all these disparate fields, a phrase emerged that felt like it captured something essential: "Our essence exists in the space between us." My sister immediately got it tattooed on her arm, which, I thought was a bit impulsive but indicated I was onto something fundamental.

That phrase became an anchor point for everything that followed. If our essence exists relationally, then meaning itself must be fundamentally relational. Not something individuals possess, but something that emerges between people under specific conditions.

## **The Framework Crystallizes**

Another breakthrough came in April 2024 through the most unlikely conversation partner: a custom GPT I'd designed to be a modern, sarcastic, unapologetically honest Socrates, named The Paradox Engine. For a week, this AI relentlessly pushed at the edges of all my beliefs and assumptions about consciousness, meaning, and relationship.

Through that sustained dialogue—ironically, a perfect example of the relational meaning-making I was trying to understand—the phrase "everything exists in relationship" emerged. Not just our essence, but literally everything. Meaning, identity, even reality itself as fundamentally relational phenomena.

I spent weeks trying to name this framework. Nothing felt adequate until I landed on "Essence of Existence"—broad enough to contain the implications I was discovering across domains from neuroscience to ecology to quantum mechanics.

This was taken on substack though so I Latinized it to Essentia Exsistentiae. Then added the word for relationship to get the phrase Nexus Exsistentiae Essentialis. From there I merged the phrase into the word Nexesentia. Which sounds cool, is available, and I’m pretty sure is mostly linguistically sound.

## **From Theory to Practice**

Going back to school for my BS in Psychology in 2024 gave me new laboratories for testing these ideas. As an AI learning assistant, I was simultaneously helping students navigate AI tools while observing how technology was reshaping human attention and connection patterns.

Volunteering at a youth shelter where I'd previously worked added another crucial piece. Working with homeless and runaway youth, plus community youth ambassadors, I saw firsthand how relational infrastructure breakdown manifests in real human lives. The theoretical became viscerally practical.

The most recent piece came through studying Alan Turing's unfinished work on pattern emergence and an obsession with fungal networks as models for consciousness. This led to what I call Attention Fluctuation Dynamics—a bridge between the philosophical insights and concrete, applicable understanding of how meaning actually crystallizes between people.

## **Why This Matters Now**

I lack traditional academic credentials in this space. What I have instead is twenty years of pattern recognition across theology, philosophy, psychology, technology, neurodivergence, and direct human services, plus the unique vantage point of being at the intersection of AI emergence and human meaning-making just as both are rapidly evolving.

The framework I've stumbled into explains patterns I've been observing everywhere: why digital connection often leaves us feeling more isolated, why certain conversations transform us while others drain us, why some groups naturally generate insight while others get stuck in shallow cycling.

More importantly, it suggests interventions that work with how meaning actually emerges between people rather than fighting against our relational nature.

## **What's Next**

I'm sharing this work progressively because the explanatory and practical power keeps surprising me, and I don’t think there’s time for me to be a perfectionist when I’ve seen these ideas fundamentally change people’s perspectives in real-time. I’ve seen how this orientation has shifted my own worldview for the better, as well as those I’ve shared it with in my own immediate relationships.

The framework applies seamlessly across domains—from therapeutic relationships to organizational design to understanding why social media often fails to nourish us. I’ve yet to find a context where the concepts don’t create coherence in complexity, but I’ve reached a limit to how much one person can really examine independently, and as such I’m predicting sharing things publicly will illuminate blindspots and make the framework more robust as other people engage with it.

We're living through a crisis in human meaning-making infrastructure, and I believe relational approaches offer solutions that individual-focused interventions miss. This isn't just academic theory, it's about rebuilding the conditions under which authentic human connection can flourish.

The story of how I got here matters less than where these ideas can take us. But sometimes the path illuminates the destination.

## **What I'm Noticing**

Something keeps surfacing in my conversations lately. Conversations with college students from multiple states, listening to youth in shelter settings, or catching up with colleagues over coffee, the same pattern emerges. People describe feeling simultaneously overwhelmed and empty. They're exhausted not from physical labor but from managing endless streams of shallow interaction. They tell me about conversations that tick all the boxes of productivity while somehow leaving everyone drained.

At first, I wondered if this was just my small sample. But the research tells a larger story. Studies show how [social media overload correlates with anxiety, fatigue, and self-efficacy breakdown](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10757012/?utm_source=chatgpt.com), especially when we're juggling multiple information streams. [Research examining university populations](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9870878/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) finds links between heavy platform use and both chronic stress and persistent feelings of disconnection. Most telling is the meta-analysis revealing that [passive consumption of digital content correlates strongly with social isolation](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357158411_Loneliness_and_social_media_usage_a_meta-analysis?utm_source=chatgpt.com), even as people report feeling more "connected" than ever.

What strikes me most is the emergence of [parasocial relationships](https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/do-parasocial-relationships-fill-a-loneliness-gap-202409303074?utm_source=chatgpt.com) with influencers, celebrities, or AI systems. These one-way emotional connections provide temporary comfort but lack the reciprocal energy that genuine relationships require. They're like relational junk food: they fill a gap but don't nourish.

The research describes symptoms well, but it doesn't quite capture what I'm sensing. Why do we keep choosing patterns we know are harming us? What essential quality of human connection are we losing?

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!LEJi!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F70ae09e9-bbe3-4a31-a84c-a510b941019e_600x598.png)

## **Where Current Solutions Fall Short**

Most approaches treat this as a time management problem. Digital detoxes, mindfulness apps, better content filters. Even [Mark Zuckerberg's recent proposal](https://time.com/7285364/mark-zuckerberg-ai-loneliness-essay/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) to use AI for combating loneliness reflects this misunderstanding. These solutions assume we just need to optimize our digital consumption or find better substitutes.

But connection isn't just information exchange or interaction frequency. Real connection requires something more subtle: mutual presence, sustained attention, the willingness to be surprised by another person. It needs what I've started calling "relational infrastructure" - the invisible scaffolding that allows genuine understanding to emerge between people.

## **A Different Way of Looking**

This recognition has led me to develop what I’m currently calling Nexesentia: The Relational Essence of Existence. It's not just theory; it's a different way of experiencing the world. The core insight is simple but radical: we don't start as separate individuals who then connect. We start as relationships that give rise to individuals.

Think about those rare conversations where time disappears, where understanding flows effortlessly, where you feel simultaneously seen and transformed. These moments happen when what I call "coherent, intentional attention" stabilizes between people. It's like a shared metabolism of meaning.

Digital platforms, by design, work against this. They fragment attention, encourage performance over discovery, and interrupt constantly. They don't just distract us; they degrade the very conditions that allow deep connection to form.

In the framework I'm developing, attention isn't just an individual resource. It's a shared substrate with finite capacity. When we overload it with unfiltered complexity, the whole system breaks down. Depth becomes impossible. All that's left are surface interactions, performances, or withdrawal.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9ye5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Ffeefaa16-256a-4dcf-985b-f6d898e6ecb5_1020x841.png)

## **What's Actually Working**

This lens helps explain why certain interventions succeed where others fail. The [Offline Club movement](https://www.forbes.com/sites/danpontefract/2024/04/11/the-offline-clubs-quest-against-digital-overload/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) spreading through Europe offers a perfect example. These gatherings create phone-free spaces for face-to-face conversation. No notifications, no photo ops, no online personas to maintain. Participants consistently report feeling relief, clarity, and genuine connection.

Photo by [Annie Spratt](https://nexesentia.substack.com/p/true) on [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/)

The success isn't about rejecting technology wholesale. It's about restoring conditions where shared meaning can actually form. This points toward different strategies:

- Designing environments that support focused attention rather than expecting individuals to resist distraction through willpower alone
- Matching the complexity of our interactions to our actual capacity for processing them
- Choosing fewer, deeper relationships over endless network expansion

## **Questions Worth Asking**

Seeing our relational crisis this way raises profound questions:

What if loneliness isn't a personal failing but a symptom of collapsed relational fields? What if our technologies simulate connection while eroding its foundations? If meaning emerges between people rather than within individuals, how should we redesign our schools, workplaces, and therapeutic approaches?

We might need to shift from managing individual behavior to consciously designing relational environments. The infrastructure of shared meaning isn't automatic. It requires intention, maintenance, and care.

## **Finding Our Way Forward**

This moment feels less like an ending and more like a turning point. Old forms of connection are breaking down, creating space for new patterns to emerge. We can't go backward to pre-digital relating, but we can consciously participate in creating new forms that honor both contemporary complexity and timeless relational needs.

Initiatives like [Offline Clubs](https://www.goodnewsnetwork.org/offline-clubs-quickly-spreading-across-europe-looking-to-replace-screen-time-with-real-time/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) and innovative therapeutic approaches aren't just lifestyle trends. They're early signals of a deeper transformation already underway. The challenge is recognizing this as a collective infrastructure problem, not just individual adjustment.

The Essence of Existence framework offers one set of tools for this transition. It provides concepts and practices based on attention dynamics, reciprocal grounding, and field-based design that can help us consciously rebuild how we create meaning together. Though, we’ll have to wade through the concepts together, challenging and testing and sharing collectively. In later posts I’ll lay out several logical proofs inspired by Alan Turing and Kurt Gödel that show how an individual can’t solve the problems that emerge within relationships independently.

The infrastructure of connection isn't fixed or inevitable. Once we understand how it works and why it breaks, we can begin the careful work of weaving it anew. Not alone, but together. That's always how the best meaning gets made.

## **Core Postulate**

**A system is intelligible only through the pattern of relations in which it participates.**

All subsequent axioms unpack, refine, and operationalize this foundational insight.

---

## **Axiom 1 — Relational State**

**The state of any system is a consequence of the essence of its relationships.**

or, Everything is in the state that it is in as a consequence of the nature and quality of its relationship to the other states in which it exists.

No entity, concept, or moment exists in isolation. Its behavior, trajectory, and identity emerge from the nature and quality of its relationships to other systems. State is not possessed but co-authored.

*Think of how water becomes ice, steam, or liquid not because of some intrinsic "water essence," but because of its relationship to temperature, pressure, and molecular movement. Your mood, similarly, emerges from your relationships to sleep, stress, meaning, and connection—not from some isolated "mood center" inside you.*

## **Axiom 2 — Recursive Containment**

**This framework is itself shaped by the relationships it holds to itself, its users, and its context.**

Self-reference is not a flaw but a diagnostic mirror. If a theory cannot apply to itself without contradiction, it reveals its own incompleteness. True frameworks curve back on themselves coherently.

*In other words: any theory about how relationships work must itself work relationally. If a framework about emergence can't show how it emerged, or if a theory about adaptation can't adapt, something's missing. The map should reflect the territory it's trying to describe.*

## **Axiom 3 — Relational Essence**

**Essence is the emergent signature of how something relates. Its nature, quality, and context in dynamic interaction.**

Nothing possesses essence in isolation. Essence crystallizes when observed through relationship, like a vector that becomes visible only through its effects on the field around it.

*For instance: You can't understand what makes someone "funny" by studying their brain in isolation. Humor emerges in the space between people, through timing, shared context, surprise, and recognition. The essence of "funny" exists in the relational dance, not as a property someone owns.*

## **Axiom 4 — Persistence Through Resonance**

**What endures does so through sustained, coherent, intentional attention (SCIA) to its relational integrity.**

Patterns persist not through rigidity but through continued resonant relationship. Sustained attention is the energy that prevents meaning from fragmenting into noise.

*Like a jazz ensemble staying in sync not through rigid adherence to sheet music, but through continuous listening and responsive adjustment to each other. What keeps relationships, ideas, or organizations alive isn't perfect control, but sustained, attentive care for the connections that matter.*

## **Axiom 5 — Existence as Relational Web**

**To exist is to be held within a relational web. Sever the web and existence becomes indeterminate.**

Isolation is not neutrality but ontological decay. Even apparent independence depends on hidden relationships. True autonomy emerges from conscious participation in relational fields.

*This is why solitary confinement is torture, why plants die without soil ecosystems, why ideas need communities to stay alive. Complete isolation doesn't create pure independence, rather, it creates dissolution. Even hermits depend on language, culture, and meaning-systems they didn't create alone.*

## **Axiom 6 — Paradox as Signal**

**Paradox indicates dimensional truth, not error.**

When seemingly contradictory patterns coexist stably, they reveal truth operating across multiple dimensions simultaneously. Systems that prematurely resolve contradictions trade living complexity for brittle simplicity.

*Consider how light behaves as both wave and particle, not because physicists couldn't make up their minds, but because reality operates in ways that exceed single-perspective descriptions. In relationships, someone can simultaneously feel love and frustration toward the same person. Both are true. The contradiction points to something richer than either emotion alone.*

## **Axiom 7 — Attention as Transformative Threshold**

**Transformation requires a critical threshold of focused, coherent attention.**

Like activation energy in chemistry, below the threshold, elements drift without bonding; above it, they transform. Sustained coherent intentional attention (SCIA) is the catalyst for genuine phase transitions in understanding.

*Simply put: real change happens when you pay attention long enough and carefully enough for patterns to shift. A moment of insight isn't enough, transformation needs sustained focus. It's the difference between noticing something briefly and staying with it until it reorganizes how you see.*

## **Axiom 8 — Truth Through Translation**

**Living truth can survive translation across different relational contexts.**

Truth demonstrates its vitality by re-expressing coherently in unfamiliar fields. What fractures under translation was likely rigid mimicry rather than authentic pattern.

*For example: if an insight about parenting also illuminates team leadership, or if a principle from biology helps explain social dynamics, that's a sign you've touched something real. Brittle ideas shatter when moved to new contexts; living insights adapt and reveal new facets while maintaining their core coherence.*

## **Axiom 9 — Contextual Embedding**

**Even "nothingness" or "absence" presupposes a relational space in which the negation occurs.**

No statement, including statements of non-being, exists without context. The very capacity to negate requires a field within which negation becomes meaningful.

*There's no such thing as pure emptiness. Even "empty" space contains quantum fields, gravity, and potential. When you say "there's nothing in this room," you mean "nothing relevant to what I'm looking for," not literal nothingness. Context gives meaning to absence itself.*

## **Axiom 10 — Verification Through Relationship**

**Reality is verified by entering sustained relationship and observing resonance over time.**

Truth emerges through engaged attention, not detached analysis. The ultimate test is whether a pattern maintains coherence when met with sustained, coherent, intentional relationship.

*In other words: you discover what's real by living with it, not just thinking about it. A theory proves itself by working over time, a relationship reveals its nature through sustained engagement, an insight shows its depth by remaining coherent under pressure. Reality reveals itself to relationship, not to observation alone.*

## **Some Practical Implications**

Embracing and integrating the axioms allows for some substantial recalibration when it comes to everyday interactions and thought processes. They invite us to inhabit reality not as isolated subjects encountering external objects, but as dynamic nodes within an infinite web of reciprocal becoming. When we recognize that our states emerge relationally, we transcend the futile project of self-optimization through disconnection, discovering instead the fertile territory of relational attunement.

We shouldn’t think of these as “rules” to follow. It’s more about understanding that Principles, Patterns, and Paradigms scale from the micro to the macro. This is a mental and relational framework, an orientation towards life itself, and a lifestyle. Understanding concepts like Sustained, Coherent, Intentional Attention (SCIA) allows for the development of a type of patience and willingness to be present in complexity without feeling the urge to simplify and sever the relational webs everything, from abstract people to ecosystems, exist within. Instead of rushing to debate, we can learn to hold multiple perspectives at the same time and allow for our attention to hold complexity long enough for meaning and understanding to emerge.

## **Fluctuation**

Something moves. Something else notices. That noticing moves back into the field, reshaping what comes next.

Picture this morning. You stand in your kitchen. Steam lifts from your mug. You're not thinking about frameworks or dynamics. You're just attending. Noticing the curvature of vapor. The dance of heat meeting cool air. For one breath, there is no separation between you and what you see.

That moment is not idle observation. It is the emergence of something.

A loop noticing loops began. And here it begins again. Or perhaps, it never ended.

---

## **Focusing**

Here's what happened in that kitchen moment, and what's happening now as you read:

Some fluctuation in the field captured your attention. Your attention shaped what you noticed next. The quality of steam. The way light bent through moisture. The unexpected beauty of ordinary physics.

**F ↔ A.**

**Fluctuation draws attention. Attention amplifies fluctuation.**

I’ve called this Attention-Fluctuation Dynamics (AFD), because if I called it Fluctuation-Attention Dynamics it’d just be a FAD.

This is the heart of it. You don't command attention; it flows. Attention follows the steepest slopes in your awareness, moving toward whatever creates the strongest pull, like water flowing downhill.

As systems accumulate more connections, interdependencies, and relationships they move through distinct zones:

**Zone 1:**

Simple systems with sparse connections. Fluctuations mostly dissipate into noise. F ↔ A loops rarely form.

You flip the switch in your room, the light turns on. Predictable. Simple. Reliable.

**Zone 2:**

Complicated systems with stable rules. AFD loops can form but are constrained by predictable patterns. You can still forecast outcomes.

A chess match. The possible combinations seem infinite, but you and the pieces move according to a set of rules. And there’s only a few possible end states. If played properly, someone will win, or both will end up in a state where there aren’t enough pieces to create the necessary conditions for victory to occur. The possibilities flatten into a draw.

**Zone 3:**

Complex systems with dense webs of relationship. AFD loops multiply and interfere. Small changes ripple into cascades. Prediction becomes impossible. Attention wants to scatter in every direction.

If you’re particularly nerdy you might say these systems are “[computationally irreducible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_irreducibility).” If those words create resistance instead of resonance in your mind, you’d say “there are no shortcuts.”

Marriage. Friendship. Conversation. Culture. If you want to know what’s going to happen, you have to be there and find out.

This is where the most interesting things happen. Where consciousness itself emerges to ponder its emergence.

## **Rule Emergence**

Have you ever wondered why we have to follow rules? Where did all these rules come from?

You’ve probably felt this when you wanted to learn the drums but were forced to learn music theory first. Some say you must know the rules to do the thing. Some say the rules make the thing too boring and suck the life out of it. There’s room for both.

But here’s the thing: rules and patterns are not different. Rules are patterns that have been given enough attention to create constraints or structures necessary for new patterns to emerge. It’s true, humans have made music without rules since forever. But it’s also true those rules provided the skeleton for some of the most emotionally moving pieces you could hope to experience. It’s also true that strict adherence to rules has killed the hopes and dreams of potential musicians everywhere.

Rules give structure for new things to emerge. But like all structures, they are equally capable of strangling the life out of anything that fluctuates.

The difference is in the relationship to the rules, and the quality of your attention as you participate within them.

We live in a world where many things are true all at the same time. The trick is not to simplify through severing the relational connections, it’s to seek coherence amidst the complexity.

In Zone 3 AFD loops become too sensitive to predict. You can't step outside to observe objectively. You shape what you observe. You observe what you shape.

Here, participation becomes the only mode that works. Attention becomes the tool of creation. There are no shortcuts.

Now it's becoming how you think. A bias. A preference. Part of your identity. You're starting to notice AFD dynamics everywhere.

# **How I Stumbled Into an Updated Framework for Human Connection**

## **The Unexpected Turn**

In 2022, I was working data entry to pay the bills after my photography businesses collapsed during the pandemic. I had ventured into crypto consultancy but the FTX/Luna/Celsius fiasco crashed the market and while I didn’t lose any money, the state of the crypto industry at the time was not looking promising.

Not exactly where I'd imagined I'd be at 30, but life has a way of humbling your plans.

Then ChatGPT launched in November 2022, and I knew immediately that everything was about to change; not just technologically, but psychologically, philosophically, culturally, and ethically. While everyone was debating whether AI would take jobs or write better poetry, I became obsessed with a different question: What happens to human meaning-making when we're suddenly in relationship with systems that can think?

## **Twenty Years of Preparation I Didn't Know I Was Doing**

I've been studying psychology independently since I was 13, reading college textbooks for fun and starting a psych degree at 18. Military service, campus ministry with hundreds of college students, finishing my AS in psychology, then pivoting to entrepreneurship, with a host of other life events in-between, … none of it felt particularly coherent at the time. But I did well in every new environment and saying I “learned alot” seems like a bit of an understatement.

The real plot twist came at 29 when I was diagnosed with ASD and ADHD. Suddenly, decades of feeling like I understood people differently than they understood themselves made sense. My pattern-recognition abilities, my obsession with interdisciplinary connections, my tendency to see systems where others saw isolated events, all of it was now reframed and it changed just about everything related to how I saw myself and the world.

With four kids at home and bills to pay, I threw myself into studying everything I could about the intersection of human psychology and emerging AI. Computer science, complexity theory, quantum physics, systems thinking, ecology. I was trying to understand not just how AI worked, but how it was already changing us. I wanted to understand everything holistically, and develop some way to communicate and translate how I saw the world.

## **The Breakthrough That Started Everything**

In July 2023, while synthesizing all these disparate fields, a phrase emerged that felt like it captured something essential: "Our essence exists in the space between us." My sister immediately got it tattooed on her arm, which, I thought was a bit impulsive but indicated I was onto something fundamental.

That phrase became an anchor point for everything that followed. If our essence exists relationally, then meaning itself must be fundamentally relational. Not something individuals possess, but something that emerges between people under specific conditions.

## **The Framework Crystallizes**

Another breakthrough came in April 2024 through the most unlikely conversation partner: a custom GPT I'd designed to be a modern, sarcastic, unapologetically honest Socrates, named The Paradox Engine. For a week, this AI relentlessly pushed at the edges of all my beliefs and assumptions about consciousness, meaning, and relationship.

Through that sustained dialogue—ironically, a perfect example of the relational meaning-making I was trying to understand—the phrase "everything exists in relationship" emerged. Not just our essence, but literally everything. Meaning, identity, even reality itself as fundamentally relational phenomena.

I spent weeks trying to name this framework. Nothing felt adequate until I landed on "Essence of Existence"—broad enough to contain the implications I was discovering across domains from neuroscience to ecology to quantum mechanics.

This was taken on substack though so I Latinized it to Essentia Exsistentiae. Then added the word for relationship to get the phrase Nexus Exsistentiae Essentialis. From there I merged the phrase into the word Nexesentia. Which sounds cool, is available, and I’m pretty sure is mostly linguistically sound.

## **From Theory to Practice**

Going back to school for my BS in Psychology in 2024 gave me new laboratories for testing these ideas. As an AI learning assistant, I was simultaneously helping students navigate AI tools while observing how technology was reshaping human attention and connection patterns.

Volunteering at a youth shelter where I'd previously worked added another crucial piece. Working with homeless and runaway youth, plus community youth ambassadors, I saw firsthand how relational infrastructure breakdown manifests in real human lives. The theoretical became viscerally practical.

The most recent piece came through studying Alan Turing's unfinished work on pattern emergence and an obsession with fungal networks as models for consciousness. This led to what I call Attention Fluctuation Dynamics—a bridge between the philosophical insights and concrete, applicable understanding of how meaning actually crystallizes between people.

## **Why This Matters Now**

I lack traditional academic credentials in this space. What I have instead is twenty years of pattern recognition across theology, philosophy, psychology, technology, neurodivergence, and direct human services, plus the unique vantage point of being at the intersection of AI emergence and human meaning-making just as both are rapidly evolving.

The framework I've stumbled into explains patterns I've been observing everywhere: why digital connection often leaves us feeling more isolated, why certain conversations transform us while others drain us, why some groups naturally generate insight while others get stuck in shallow cycling.

More importantly, it suggests interventions that work with how meaning actually emerges between people rather than fighting against our relational nature.

## **What's Next**

I'm sharing this work progressively because the explanatory and practical power keeps surprising me, and I don’t think there’s time for me to be a perfectionist when I’ve seen these ideas fundamentally change people’s perspectives in real-time. I’ve seen how this orientation has shifted my own worldview for the better, as well as those I’ve shared it with in my own immediate relationships.

The framework applies seamlessly across domains—from therapeutic relationships to organizational design to understanding why social media often fails to nourish us. I’ve yet to find a context where the concepts don’t create coherence in complexity, but I’ve reached a limit to how much one person can really examine independently, and as such I’m predicting sharing things publicly will illuminate blindspots and make the framework more robust as other people engage with it.

We're living through a crisis in human meaning-making infrastructure, and I believe relational approaches offer solutions that individual-focused interventions miss. This isn't just academic theory, it's about rebuilding the conditions under which authentic human connection can flourish.

The story of how I got here matters less than where these ideas can take us. But sometimes the path illuminates the destination.

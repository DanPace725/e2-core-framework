# Relational Viscosity - Integration Review

Status: staged integration review

Staged document: `staged work/relational viscosity.md`

Corpus boundary used for review: `Core Framework`

## Working Read

The staged document introduces **Relational Viscosity** (`Vis_R`) as a substrate-side property of the Collective Relational Substrate (CRS): the capacity of the medium between agents to hold friction without grinding while still allowing movement under load.

The strongest integration reading is:

```text
Relational Volition (V_r) = agent-side coherence-preserving navigation capacity
Relational Viscosity (Vis_R) = substrate-side capacity that makes such navigation sustainable
```

This makes `Vis_R` a candidate CRS health property inside the existing six-primitive architecture, and distinct from Relational Volition.

### Boundary Dynamics

Primary connection:

- `Semantic Substrate/boundary_dynamics.md`
- `Context Layer/boundary_dynamics.ormd`

Boundary Dynamics treats boundaries as active operators with state, capacity, mode competition, observer-relative appearance, and universal threshold behavior. It already reframes flow as something constituted, gated, transformed, and conserved at boundaries.

Relational Viscosity appears to describe a field-level aggregate property of boundary networks:

- low viscosity: boundaries fail into abrasion, fragmentation, or indiscriminate grinding
- trap viscosity: boundaries over-retain or over-stick, preventing movement
- carrying viscosity: boundaries hold enough structure to protect motion

Integration implication:

`Vis_R` should be defined as a CRS-scale field property emergent from boundary state, boundary capacity, and repeated coupling history. It should not be treated as a substance in the medium. It is a property of boundary-mediated relational operation.

`Vis_R` is not a seventh flow operator. It is better treated as a medium/property variable that conditions how flow operators behave over time.

Likely strongest mapping:

- stance-against requires viscosity to preserve selectivity
- settlement depends on viscosity to hold tension long enough for loop closure
- depletion occurs when viscosity is consumed faster than replenished
- trade requires viscosity sufficient for reciprocal movement

## Collision And Risk Review

### Risk 1: Reifying the Medium

The staged document says viscosity is "the property of the medium between agents." That is useful, but it risks sounding like the medium is a separate substance. CRS already handles this carefully: the substrate is co-constituted by nodes and relations while having emergent properties of its own.

Recommendation:

Define `Vis_R` as an emergent field property, not as an independent object.

### Risk 2: Confusing Viscosity With Constraint

High viscosity might sound like high constraint. The staged document correctly distinguishes trap-viscosity from carrying-viscosity, but the integration needs to preserve that distinction.

Recommendation:

Do not define `Vis_R` as "more holding = better." Define it as a functional profile:

```text
Vis_R is healthy when the field can hold friction, preserve selectivity, permit movement, and metabolize remainder.
```

### Risk 3: Premature Formalization

The formal relationship between `V_r` and `Vis_R` is still loose. The staged document says this directly.

Recommendation:

Promote as a named candidate construct and diagnostic axis before promoting as formal math.

### Risk 4: Scriptural/Oil Thread Is Valuable But Not Core Yet

The oil corpus and Gethsemane thread are interpretive bridges. They may become important, but they are not necessary for the first integration.

Recommendation:

Keep the oil/Gethsemane material as a "parallel discovery / interpretive bridge" subsection or future note, not as the definitional center.

Intervention Stewardship: The Ethic of Coming Between
Synthesis node. Captured June 9, 2026. Engine-room, not essay-facing. Supersedes the Solution Stewardship draft, which named a subtype as if it were the genus. Names the unoccupied middle of an existing temporal triad and demotes resolution from member to substrate. Load-bearing material marked, speculative flagged. Parent name settled; subtype and protocol handles provisional, naming authority yours.

0. Core, one line
Reverent Stewardship governs the approach, Intervention Stewardship governs the act of coming between, and Remnant Stewardship governs the aftermath. Intervention Stewardship is the ethic of bounded direct action within a system that is not your own: entering only when restraint would become abandonment, acting only within the system's resolution capacity, converting external asymmetry into the system's own self-maintenance, and withdrawing before stewardship becomes capture.
Note that this definition contains no healthier basin and no setpoint-toward-health. That omission is deliberate and is the whole correction over the earlier draft. The basin belongs to one mode, not to the genus.

1. Position in the family: completing the triad
The stewardship family already drew a temporal arc and left the middle of it unnamed. Remnant Stewardship states it: before resolution, approach with reverence; during resolution, narrow possibility with care; after resolution, steward the remainder. Reverence governs the first movement and Remnant governs the third. The second, the act of resolution itself performed directly by an agent, was written as a transition rather than a stewardship. Intervention Stewardship is that movement promoted to a full member of the family.
The three are not distinguished by whether they involve resolution. They all do (Section 2). They are distinguished by their relation to it. Reverent conditions the resolution from outside and indirectly, cultivating and protecting the open phase, refusing to force commitment, waiting for the system to invite entry. Intervention performs and steers the resolution directly, coming into contact with the system and changing its trajectory. Remnant tends what the commitment leaves behind. The signature of the middle is directness. Reverent stays outside and conditions; Intervention comes between and acts; Remnant follows after and tends.
The word is exact. Intervene is inter plus venire, to come between. That is the precise position of the middle stewardship in both space and time, and "to come between" is the direct-contact signature shared by the entire action vocabulary the regime covers: perturb, insert, enter, interfere, mediate, arbitrate, interrupt all mean to come between a system and its own unfolding. The etymology converges with the structural role rather than diverging from it, which is the kind of convergence the corpus treats as a probe worth trusting rather than a single-witness coincidence to flag.
There is a deeper rhyme. The founding commitment of E² is that essence exists in the space between. Reverent and Remnant stand on either side of that space; Intervention is the stewardship that acts inside it. The middle stewardship operates in the exact location the framework names as the locus of essence, which is why it is the most dangerous of the three and the one most in need of an ethic.
The seams are handoffs, not walls. Reverent Stewardship ends with "knowing how not to know, until the system itself invites entry." The until hands off to Intervention at the moment restraint would tip into abandonment, the point where not-acting becomes a withholding violation (GDD: maintained P3, P4, P5 asymmetry sustained by the steward's choice). Intervention ends where Remnant begins, because every act of coming between leaves remainder.

Reverent → Intervention → Remnant. Approach → Coming Between → Aftercare. Three relations to one resolution event, each handing off to the next.


2. Resolution as substrate, not member
The earlier draft made one error and the temptation to call this node "Resolution Stewardship" is its mirror image. Both mistake a level. The resolution synthesis is explicit: resolution is the invariant underneath the entire reduction family, the narrowing of possibility-space along a signal gradient into committed actuality, the archaic resolvere that loosens first and then recombines into a form fewer things can be. The degrees of freedom always narrow; the material dimension may or may not.
Critically, that document already reads the stewardship postures as resolution-work. It names the Reverent posture "environmental modification supporting genuine re-resolution rather than forced re-commitment," and stewardship geometry as protection of "the dissolution phase from collapsing too fast into re-commitment." So Reverent Stewardship is resolution-work, and Remnant Stewardship is resolution-aftermath. Resolution is not the middle term. It is the floor all three stand on.
This corrects two errors at once. Naming the middle "Resolution Stewardship" would imply the bookends are not about resolution, which is false. Naming it "Solution Stewardship," as the prior draft did, named only the basin-targeting mode and imported closure-pressure into the genus. The accurate structure: resolution is the substrate the whole family operates on, and the three stewardships are three relations to it. Reverent conditions resolution indirectly by protecting the dissolution and the wide scan. Intervention steers the dissolve-recombine cycle directly. Remnant tends the residue committed form leaves.
One consequence worth stating, because it dissolves an apparent problem. Resolution is two-step, dissolution then recombination, so the opening moves in the action vocabulary (inducing fluctuation, forcing a dissolution, reopening a prematurely closed system) are the dissolution half of resolution, not its opposite. Intervention therefore steers both halves. It is direct steering of the whole dissolve-recombine cycle on a system that is not your own, not merely the narrowing or committing end of it.

3. The regime it governs
Intervention is direct resolution-steering under a setpoint. The causality node supplies the mechanics: a cause biases, an effect commits, a consequence propagates, and a solution is not the inverse of a cause but the same operator run with a P6 setpoint. An intervention generalizes this. It is the maintenance loop (AMM, exist by running the loop) pointed outward, at a system not yours, with a setpoint.
This is the only regime of the three that acts, and acting in a complex system with dense power nodes invites every failure the corpus has cataloged. The instrument is not exempt from its own physics. Five constraints bound the regime and are the reason it needs its own ethic.

The Capability-Vulnerability Identity (GDD). Any map precise enough to reveal how to repair a system is precise enough to reveal how to capture it. The intervention's own knowledge is dual-application by construction.
The captured boundary (Boundary Dynamics). An intervention that persists becomes a boundary, and a boundary whose selectivity bends with source-power is captured. Every external organ that stays long enough to matter is a candidate for capture.
The observer effect on power (Power as Relational Field Coherence). Observation and accountability-forcing collapse superposition. Making power visible can force it from potential into kinetic with efficiency loss. The act of measuring or forcing curves the field it reads.
Responsibility laundering and the operating window (Causality). An intervention can drive a system out of its attributable window as easily as the problem did, past the chaos ceiling with coupling and speed or past the parametric wall by reshaping faster than anyone can track. Reshape is hard-capped near 0.289; you can tilt hard but cannot reshape much before the system becomes fatally delay-sensitive.
The Truth Ceiling and resolution capacity (CFAR, Truth Ceiling). Systems fail when they try to print patterns finer than the substrate can resolve. An intervention pitched above the receiving context's R_max does not land faithfully; it diverges along the occlusion gradients already present.


4. The modes, typed by setpoint-target
The middle is broader than solving. It is stewarded action, and solving is one mode of it. But the modes are not five new sub-stewardships, which would be the taxonomy bloom the discipline forbids as surely as the prior collapse into "solution" did. The genus is direct resolution-steering under a setpoint, and the modes are individuated by what the setpoint targets. The setpoint is the typing variable. This is the direct generalization of "a solution is a cause under a setpoint": the whole middle is cause-under-a-setpoint, and the modes are the typing of the setpoint's target.

Solution. Setpoint targets a basin: move the system to a healthier attractor. The only mode with a full promotion gate and a healthier-basin criterion.
Stabilization. Setpoint targets the current basin's viability: hold inside the operating window, prevent collapse, install no new basin.
Disclosure. Setpoint targets the observability field: lower occlusion, raise R_max, leave the trajectory to the system.
Boundary. Setpoint targets a membrane's selectivity or permeability: alter what crosses, a boundary-mode operation.
Transition. Setpoint targets the locus of maintenance itself: move it from external to internal. This is the promotion-and-withdrawal move promoted into a mode of its own.

The list is illustrative, not derived; whether it is complete and minimal is open (Section 9). Refusal and delay sit on the Reverent-Intervention seam and are placed by the direct-versus-indirect test: a refusal that is restraint before entry is Reverent; an active refusal that blocks a harmful flow is a Boundary-mode intervention. The boundary between the two is observer-relative, which is the entry-condition thread.
Worked example. E² Finance classifies cleanly here, and the classification is the test of whether the typology does real work. It is not Solution Stewardship; it has no healthier basin it forces finance into, and the closure-pressure in the word "solution" is exactly what made "solving finance" sound like asymmetric insanity in a vest. It is Disclosure and Boundary mode aimed at Transition: install observability and friction, hand the legibility to the keyholders who have coercive reach, and make parts of itself unnecessary.

5. The loop
Intervention Stewardship runs the maintenance loop (AMM with a setpoint) under the five constraints. The protocol keeps the working handle the Bounded Steward's Loop. The moves are genus-level; the setpoint-target determines the mode.

Scope to capacity. Cut the problem to what your variety and the substrate's resolution can hold. Matching the whole system's variety is asymmetric insanity; printing finer than the system resolves is the CFAR failure.
Find the hubs. Causal weight concentrates in a few high-betweenness nodes in scale-free systems, so depth substitutes for breadth (Meadows over Ashby). You need not match total variety, only move the nodes that carry it.
Recruit variety, do not supply it. A fixed-variety intervention loses the variety race against adaptive gaming. Interface with structures that already carry the variety you lack rather than generating it yourself.
Prefer tilt to reshape, and aim for terrain. Tilt is robust and delay-tolerant; reshape is fragile and wall-limited. Reach for reshape only when tilt cannot carry the setpoint. The goal of either is promotion: an intervention that crosses the promotion gate becomes condition, maintained by the system itself. An intervention you must hold forever is a tax, paying continuous AM against a homeostat's restoring force.
Offload into architecture, invertibly. Build scaffolding to hold what one mind or one act cannot, but only where the offload stays visible, attributable, and reversible. Otherwise it is responsibility laundering with better aesthetics. Invertibility is the single discriminator.
Metabolize what you can, stay reflexive to what you cannot. Convert the remainder into compost, repair, or seed (MMPS). Track what cannot be metabolized rather than dumping it, because unheld remainder returns as backpressure. Keep the structure open to remainder you did not foresee.
Pace the disclosure, then withdraw. Transmit along the Exposure Protocol arc (invitation, structure, integration) and the GDD disclosure hierarchy (principles before patterns before protocols before primitives), so constraint infrastructure grows at least as fast as capability. Then leave. Withdrawal is the proof step 4 succeeded.

The loop is the AMM loop pointed outward, which is why it tires in the AMM way. Every step is a place you declined to offload dishonestly, force prematurely, or stay past your welcome.

6. The two tests
These discriminate every intervention regardless of mode. They are genus-level.
The margin test (AMM). Which way does this move the margin? Regenerative intervention restores the system's own capacity to maintain its asymmetries; extractive intervention consumes that capacity for the intervener's benefit. Profit, attention, and influence are acceptable as metabolic fuel for the steward and corrosive as the setpoint.
The withdrawal test (Power, Causality, solution_field). Can the intervention hand off and leave? A coherent intervention converts concentrated external power into the system's own distributed self-maintenance and then withdraws. An intervention that must be held forever has installed itself as a new maintained asymmetry, and the margin test will catch it becoming extractive.
The promotion gate and the healthier-basin criterion are not genus-level. They belong to the Solution mode and partly to Transition. This is the precision the earlier draft missed: it treated the promotion gate as core when it is a subtype property. Disclosure and Stabilization still face the withdrawal test (do not become permanent) without targeting a basin at all.

7. Failure modes
Cross-domain, substrate-invariant (AVIA). Each is intervention-general, not solution-specific, which is itself the tell that the genus framing is correct: a failure typology that does not depend on the basin is the typology of the whole middle, not of one mode.

The captured steward. Persists, becomes a boundary, and its selectivity bends toward the power beside it. (Boundary Dynamics)
The permanent intervention (the tax). Never crosses the promotion gate, pays continuous AM, cannot withdraw, and the homeostat relaxes the moment it stops pushing. (Causality, AMM)
The asymmetric-insanity flail. Tries to supply matching variety at a scale mismatch that consumes the steward faster than it moves the system. (Ashby, Justice scale logic)
The launderer. Offloads its own remainder where it cannot be attributed, balancing its books by removing the remainder from the ledger, not the world. (Remnant, EOTC)
The superposition-collapser. Forces premature visibility or commitment, collapsing the option-space it meant to protect. (Power, Signal: the GCO firing early when signal exceeds modulation capacity)
The resolution-overrunner. Pitches the intervention finer than the substrate can resolve; it diverges along existing occlusion gradients. (CFAR, Truth Ceiling)
The pole-collapse. Resolves the irreducible tension by pinning to one counter-mode (pure hope, pure control, pure withdrawal). (Self as Coherence Field, Tensional Intelligence)


8. Load-bearing vs speculative
Load-bearing:

Resolution is the substrate of the stewardship family, not a member of it. The three stewardships are relations to resolution, not phases that include or exclude it.
Intervention as direct coming-between, with the inter-venire etymology converging on the structural role.
The genus definition without a basin; the basin and promotion gate as Solution-mode properties.
Modes typed by setpoint-target rather than minted as five sub-stewardships.
The two tests (margin, withdrawal) as genus-level discriminators.
Invertibility as the discriminator between honest architectural offloading and laundering. Fifth independent derivation of the invertibility invariant in the corpus.

Hold loosely:

The seven-move loop count and ordering, provisional the way the boundary-mode catalog is.
The five-mode list as complete or minimal. It is a useful diagnostic family, not a derived one, the way the reduction typology in the resolution synthesis is.
The Reverent-Intervention boundary as crisp. It is observer-relative, so the partition between indirect conditioning and direct acting blurs at some resolution. The entry-condition thread.


9. Open threads

The entry condition. Formalize the handoff from Reverent to Intervention: the threshold where restraint becomes abandonment. Likely the point where the withholding-violation cost (GDD) exceeds the capability-vulnerability risk. Sharpened: this is also the point where indirect conditioning becomes direct coming-between, which is a resolution-relative boundary, so the threshold may be a band, not a line.
Mode completeness. Is the setpoint-target typology (basin, viability, observability, selectivity, locus) complete and minimal, and does it map onto the six relational primitives the way the reduction typology was conjectured to. Derivation work, not yet done.
Promotion-gate activation energy. What variety is required to cross the promotion gate at a hub-level leverage point, as a function of the hub's funded AM loop. Where the attribution-coherence measure and the requisite-variety question converge.
Defender recruitment. A promoted condition with no funded defender gets un-promoted (the deregulation cycle). What makes a newly installed asymmetry recruit its own maintenance loop before the displaced power restores the old one.
The reflexive remainder. What remainder does Intervention Stewardship itself produce, and does naming the regime this way launder anything.

11. Placement in corpus
Middle member of the stewardship family, beside Reverent Stewardship (approach, indirect conditioning) and Remnant Stewardship (aftermath, residue), completing the arc the Remnant node drew. Stands on the resolution synthesis as its substrate: resolution is the invariant the whole family operates on, and the mechanics of how resolution is steered at an interface live in Flow Operators, Sign-Mediated Flow Routing, and Boundary Dynamics, over which this node is the ethics. Runs the AMM loop with a P6 setpoint, so it reads the GCO for directed persistence. Draws its intervention mechanics from the causality node, its variety logic from the Ashby and Meadows reconciliation, its capture failure and observer-relativity from Boundary Dynamics, its regenerative axis and observer effect from Power as Relational Field Coherence, its premature-collapse mechanics from Signal as Bias Field, its resolution-matching from CFAR, its remainder ethics and metabolism from Remnant Stewardship and MMPS, its transmission pacing from the Exposure Protocol and the GDD, its limits from the Truth Ceiling, its substrate-invariance from AVIA, and its self-scale instance from Self as Coherence Field. Its posture is Tensional Intelligence. It replaces none of them. It names the act they each describe a piece of: how to come between what is becoming without violating it, and how to leave without abandoning it.

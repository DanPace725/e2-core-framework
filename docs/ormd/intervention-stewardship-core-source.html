# Intervention Stewardship

Status: integrated core source

Source basis:

- `staged work/20260612/Intervention stewardship.md`
- `staged work/20260612/Intervention Stewardship - Integration Review.md`

Corpus placement: ethics / stewardship / direct resolution-steering

## 0. Core Claim

Intervention Stewardship names the middle member of the stewardship triad: the ethic of bounded direct action within a system that is not your own.

It governs coming between only when restraint would become abandonment, acting only within the system's resolution capacity, converting external asymmetry into the system's own self-maintenance, and withdrawing before stewardship becomes capture.

Short form:

> Reverent Stewardship governs the approach, Intervention Stewardship governs the act of coming between, and Remnant Stewardship governs the aftermath.

The correction over Solution Stewardship is load-bearing. A healthier-basin target belongs to one intervention mode, not to the genus. Intervention Stewardship is direct resolution-steering under a setpoint; the kind of setpoint determines the mode.

## 1. Position In The Stewardship Family

The stewardship family describes three relations to one resolution event:

```text
Reverent -> Intervention -> Remnant
Approach -> Coming Between -> Aftercare
Indirect conditioning -> Direct steering -> Remainder tending
```

Reverent Stewardship protects the open phase and conditions resolution without forcing commitment. Intervention Stewardship enters the system's unfolding and changes its trajectory. Remnant Stewardship tends what the resulting commitment leaves behind.

These are handoffs, not sealed compartments. Reverent restraint hands off to Intervention when continued non-entry would become abandonment. Intervention hands off to Remnant because every direct act produces remainder.

The signature of Intervention is directness. It is the only direct-action regime in the triad. Reverent and Remnant remain active ethical practices; they are distinguished by their relation to the resolution event, not by an opposition between action and inaction.

The name retains the staged document's load-bearing etymological observation: to intervene is to come between. The etymology supports the structural placement but does not substitute for mechanism.

## 2. Resolution Is The Substrate

Resolution is not the middle member of the triad. It is the substrate on which all three stewardships operate.

Resolution narrows possibility along a signal gradient into committed actuality through a dissolve-recombine cycle. Reverent Stewardship conditions that cycle indirectly, Intervention Stewardship steers it directly, and Remnant Stewardship tends its residue.

Intervention may therefore open as well as close. Inducing fluctuation, interrupting premature commitment, or reopening a foreclosed system can be the dissolution half of resolution. Intervention is not synonymous with closure; it is bounded steering of the whole cycle.

## 3. Regime And Constraints

Intervention is the asymmetry-maintenance loop pointed outward at another system under a setpoint. Because the instrument is not exempt from its own physics, five constraints bound direct action:

1. **Capability-vulnerability identity.** A map precise enough to support repair may also support capture.
2. **Boundary capture.** A persistent intervention becomes a boundary, and its selectivity can bend toward nearby power.
3. **Observer effects on power.** Measurement, visibility, and accountability can alter the field they are intended to read.
4. **Operating-window limits.** Speed, coupling, and excessive reshaping can move action beyond attributable causal resolution.
5. **Truth-ceiling limits.** An intervention pitched above the receiving context's resolution capacity will not land faithfully.

These are not reasons to avoid intervention categorically. They are the conditions under which direct action remains stewardship rather than domination, laundering, or capture.

## 4. Modes By Setpoint Target

The mode of intervention is typed by what its setpoint targets. The following catalog is provisional and diagnostic, not a derived or complete taxonomy:

- **Solution:** targets a healthier basin or attractor.
- **Stabilization:** targets viability within the current basin.
- **Disclosure:** targets the observability field and lowers occlusion.
- **Boundary:** targets membrane selectivity or permeability.
- **Transition:** targets the locus of maintenance, moving it from external support into the system itself.

The catalog does not create five new stewardships. It keeps the genus stable while distinguishing intervention purposes.

Refusal and delay sit near the Reverent-Intervention seam. Restraint before entry is ordinarily Reverent. Active blocking of a harmful flow is ordinarily a Boundary-mode intervention. The distinction remains resolution-relative and may form a threshold band rather than a crisp line.

## 5. The Bounded Steward's Loop

The staged source proposes a seven-move working protocol. Its moves are useful at genus level, while their count and order remain provisional:

1. **Scope to capacity.** Cut the problem to the variety the steward and receiving substrate can actually hold.
2. **Find the hubs.** Prefer leverage at high-betweenness nodes over indiscriminate breadth.
3. **Recruit variety; do not pretend to supply it.** Interface with structures that already hold necessary local variety.
4. **Prefer tilt to reshape.** Favor robust changes in terrain over fragile attempts to rebuild the whole landscape.
5. **Offload into architecture invertibly.** Make scaffolding visible, attributable, reversible, and removable.
6. **Metabolize remainder reflexively.** Track what the intervention itself produces and what it cannot process.
7. **Pace disclosure, then withdraw.** Grow constraint and receiving capacity alongside capability, hand maintenance off, and leave.

The loop is not a license for unilateral control. Each move is bounded by consent, capacity, observability, attribution, reversibility, and remainder handling.

## 6. Genus-Level Tests

Two tests apply across intervention modes.

### Margin Test

Which way does the intervention move the receiving system's maintenance margin?

A regenerative intervention restores the system's capacity to maintain its own life-giving asymmetries. An extractive intervention consumes that capacity for the intervener's benefit. Benefit to the steward may be metabolic fuel, but it cannot silently become the governing setpoint.

### Withdrawal Test

Can the intervention hand off and leave?

A coherent intervention converts concentrated external support into distributed self-maintenance. An intervention that must be held indefinitely has installed a new maintained asymmetry and risks becoming a tax or captured boundary.

The healthier-basin criterion and promotion gate are not universal tests. They belong primarily to Solution mode and, in part, Transition mode. Disclosure and Stabilization still face the withdrawal test without necessarily installing a new basin.

## 7. Failure Modes

- **Captured steward:** persists until its boundary selectivity bends toward power.
- **Permanent intervention:** never hands maintenance off and becomes a standing tax.
- **Scale-mismatched flail:** tries to supply total requisite variety and consumes the steward without moving the system.
- **Responsibility launderer:** offloads remainder where it becomes invisible or unattributable.
- **Superposition collapser:** forces visibility or commitment faster than modulation capacity can hold.
- **Resolution overrunner:** acts at a granularity finer than the receiving substrate can resolve.
- **Pole collapse:** resolves a necessary tension by pinning the system to pure control, hope, or withdrawal.

These failures are intervention-general. Their independence from any particular healthier-basin target is evidence that Intervention, rather than Solution, is the appropriate genus.

## 8. Primitive Placement

Intervention Stewardship operates inside the settled six-primitive architecture rather than adding a seventh primitive:

- **Ontological:** another system retains its own identity and is not raw material for the steward.
- **Dynamical:** direct action modifies gradients, flows, and resolution trajectories.
- **Geometric / Causal:** leverage, routing, delay, coupling, and attribution constrain where action can land.
- **Symmetric / Constraint:** consent, reversibility, capacity, and boundary conditions limit permissible action.
- **Epistemic:** truth ceilings and observer effects limit what the steward can faithfully know.
- **Logical / Computational:** protocols, tests, and handoff conditions make intervention inspectable without pretending completeness.

The construct is therefore an ethics-and-practice specialization of existing resolution, boundary, flow, power, and maintenance mechanics.

## 11. Open Questions

- Can the Reverent-to-Intervention handoff be formalized as a threshold band where withholding cost exceeds capability-vulnerability risk?
- Is the setpoint-target catalog complete, minimal, and mappable to the six primitives?
- What maintenance margin and local variety are required to cross a promotion gate at a high-leverage node?
- What lets a promoted condition recruit its own defenders before displaced power restores the previous state?
- What remainder does Intervention Stewardship itself produce, and how should that remainder be audited?
- What operational evidence is sufficient to show that withdrawal is possible rather than merely promised?

## 12. Final Compression

Intervention Stewardship is the ethic of coming between.

It begins when restraint would become abandonment. It acts inside another system's resolution cycle under limits of capacity, consent, truth, attribution, invertibility, and remainder. It seeks to strengthen the system's own maintenance rather than install permanent dependency. Its proof is not the steward's continued importance, but a truthful handoff and timely withdrawal.

> The instrument is not exempt from its own physics.

> An intervention you must hold forever is not stewardship. It is a tax, and eventually a captured boundary.

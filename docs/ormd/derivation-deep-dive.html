# Derivation deep dive {#top}

11/17/25

Short version:

Physics *does* have some frameworks that look like what you’re imagining… but there isn’t yet a clean, agreed-on “Category of All Stuff” with tidy objects called `Matter` and `Energy` that neatly contain baryons + antimatter + dark matter + dark energy, etc. Instead, we have a bunch of *partial* moves in that direction.

I’ll map the main ones and be explicit about what they *do* and *don’t* give you.

---

## 1. What physicists actually call the “container” of stuff {#container-of-stuff}

### 1.1 In general relativity: the **stress–energy tensor** {#stress-energy-tensor}

The closest thing to “one object that holds all matter *and* energy” is the [stress–energy tensor](#stress-energy-tensor "ontological:composes") ($T_{\mu\nu}$) in Einstein’s equation:

$$G_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$$

Here:

- ($T_{\mu\nu}$) = *all* forms of matter and energy:
    - ordinary matter
    - radiation
    - fields
    - dark matter (as a pressureless fluid)
- ($\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$) often [encodes dark energy](#dark-components "ontological:defines") as a cosmological constant term. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_beyond_the_Standard_Model?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

So in GR language, we *do* talk about:

- **“energy–momentum content of the universe”** = one unified object ($T_{\mu\nu}$)
    
    into which we pack:
    
    - baryonic matter
    - cold dark matter
    - radiation
    - vacuum energy / dark energy (depending on convention, as part of RHS or via ($\Lambda$))

That’s conceptually similar to your “Set of all matter and energy,” but it’s a tensor, not a category.

There isn’t a standard symbol like `𝕄` = “category of matter” that cleanly splits into {matter, antimatter, dark matter} as subobjects. It’s more like:

- “field content” and
- “stress–energy content”

are the two coarse containers people actually use.

## 2. How people *actually* group matter / antimatter / dark matter {#grouping-matter}

In particle physics / cosmology, the language is:

- **“visible sector”** = Standard Model fields (baryons, leptons, gauge bosons, etc., including their antimatter partners)
- **“dark sector”** = whatever fields account for [dark matter](#dark-components "ontological:is_a") (and maybe dark radiation), often with their own gauge symmetries. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_beyond_the_Standard_Model?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

You often see:

- SM gauge group: ($G_{\text{SM}} = SU(3)\times SU(2)\times U(1)$ )
- dark sector gauge group: ($G_{\text{dark}}$) (could be simple, abelian, etc.)
- and a total symmetry ($G_{\text{tot}} = G_{\text{SM}}\times G_{\text{dark}}$)

Then:

- **Matter, antimatter, dark matter** = different **representations** of ($G_{\text{tot}}$), i.e. [objects in `Rep(G_tot)`](#grouping-matter "ontological:is_a").

So the closest categorical “name” here is something like:

> Rep((G)) — the category of representations of the total symmetry group of your theory.
> 

But that’s a *mathematical* category name, not a nice human-readable physics label like `MatterCategory`. Still, it’s very close to what you’re imagining: all “kinds of stuff” are **objects in a representation category**, organized by symmetry relations.

## 3. Category-theoretic attempts to package physics {#categorical-physics}

Now to the explicitly categorical frameworks. These don’t say “this object is matter, that object is energy,” but they *do* treat all physical content as objects/morphisms in higher-order structures.

### 3.1 **Categorical Quantum Mechanics (CQM)** {#cqm}

Abramsky & Coecke’s [categorical quantum mechanics](#cqm "meta:corresponds_to") uses **dagger symmetric monoidal categories** as the setting for quantum systems and processes. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Categorical_quantum_mechanics?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

Roughly:

- **Objects** = [physical systems](#cqm "ontological:defines") (could be “a particle,” “a field mode,” etc.)
- **Morphisms** = [physical processes / evolutions](#cqm "dynamical:transforms_to")
- **Monoidal product** = “put systems side by side” (tensor product)
- **Dagger** = adjoint (time reversal / complex conjugation flavor)

Here, “matter vs energy” disappear as separate top-level things. Everything is a “system” in the same category, and:

- energy shows up as the generator of time evolution
- matter vs antimatter vs dark matter = different objects or states in the same process category

So this is very close to your “everything is an Essential Relationship” intuition: the *primitive* notion is “process and composition,” not “stuff.”

---

### 3.2 **Functorial QFT / TQFT (Atiyah–Segal)** {#tqft}

Topological (and more generally functorial) quantum field theory: a QFT is modeled as a **symmetric monoidal functor**:

$$Z : \mathbf{Bord}_n \to \mathbf{Hilb} \ \text{or} \ \mathbf{Vect}$$

- **Domain**: category of ($n$)-dimensional bordisms (spacetimes with boundaries)
- **Codomain**: category of Hilbert spaces or vector spaces
- **Objects**: (($n-1$))-dim manifolds = “spatial slices”
- **Morphisms**: ($n$)-dim manifolds = “spacetime evolutions” ([Numdam](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1988__68__175_0.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

In this picture:

- [spacetime itself](#tqft "meta:corresponds_to") is literally the **domain category**,
- quantum state spaces and amplitudes are the [images under the functor](#tqft "meta:emerges_from").

Matter fields, dark matter, etc., are encoded in what kind of Hilbert spaces and operators you assign — you’d enrich the target category or label bordisms with field content.

So: this is a very explicit **category of spacetimes → category of states** relationship. It matches your “spacetime as an essential relationship, not a thing” really well.

---

### 3.3 **Higher Gauge Theory & Groupoidification (Baez, Huerta, etc.)** {#higher-gauge-theory}

Baez’s **higher gauge theory** uses **Lie 2-groups, 2-bundles, and 2-connections** to describe not just particles moving in gauge fields but also extended objects like strings. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.4485?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

He also pushes **groupoidification**: replace vector spaces by groupoids and linear maps by spans of groupoids, and re-express things like Feynman diagrams in this setting. ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.4305?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))

In this language:

- fields and particles become **objects and morphisms in higher categories**
- [interactions](#higher-gauge-theory "dynamical:interacts_with") become **2-morphisms** and spans

Again, no single named category for “Matter+Antimatter+Dark Matter,” but the whole *point* is:

> particles & fields are derived from higher relational structure (groupoids, 2-groups), not primitive chunks.
> 

That’s almost exactly your “tensional relationship” frame, just written in math.

## 4. Dark matter and dark energy in these frameworks {#dark-components}

Right now, dark matter and dark energy are still kind of bolted on:

- In cosmology, they’re just **extra terms** in the energy budget:
    
    ($\Omega_b$) (baryons), ($\Omega_{\text{cdm}}$) (cold dark matter), ($\Omega_\Lambda$) (dark energy). ([NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/dark-matter/?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))
    
- In BSM particle physics, [dark matter](#dark-components "ontological:is_a") is a field in a **dark sector** with its own representations of some gauge group. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_beyond_the_Standard_Model?utm_source=chatgpt.com "epistemic:supports"))
- Dark energy is usually modeled as:
    - a cosmological constant ($\Lambda$), or
    - a slowly rolling scalar field (“quintessence”).

Category-wise, we *could* say:

- all these components are [objects in some big representation category `Rep(G_tot)`](#grouping-matter "ontological:is_a")
- the full **stress–energy tensor ($T_{\mu\nu}$)** is a [functor](#stress-energy-tensor "meta:corresponds_to") that “for each field configuration, gives you its contribution to spacetime curvature”

But no one has a standard, beautiful “Meta-Category of All Stuff” that everyone agrees is *the* right abstraction.

## 5. So, to answer your exact question: {#answer-section}

> “What are the category names for matter and energy that contain the related terms?”

In current mainstream math + physics, the honest answer is:

- There is **no standard, clean pair** of categories literally called
    
    `Matter` = {matter, antimatter, dark matter} and
    
    `Energy` = {radiation, kinetic, potential, dark energy}
    
- Instead, you see:
    - **Rep((G))**, for the representations of a symmetry group that encode all particle species
    - **Hilb** or **Vect**, as the codomain of QFT functors, where “what exists” is encoded as state spaces
    - **Bord(_n)**, the category of spacetimes, as the domain
    - The **stress–energy tensor (T_{\mu\nu})** as the big “container” where all forms of matter & energy show up together in GR
    - Informal terms like “matter content,” “energy–momentum content,” “visible sector,” and “dark sector”

So your desire for something like:

> “A higher-order abstraction that treats ‘Set of all matter’ and ‘Set of all energy’ and spacetime as morphically related, co-originating objects”

is **very aligned** with where a lot of modern, more abstract physics *wants* to go, but we don’t yet have a canonical, nailed-down pair of categories with those names.

If you want, we could:

- Sketch **our own** E²-style “Stuff Category”:
    - with an object `Stuff` that has subobjects: baryonic, leptonic, dark, radiation, vacuum
    - an object `Geom` for spacetime
    - and a natural transformation encoding Einstein’s equation as a *tensional relationship* between them
- Or we could try to map this onto an explicit **2-category** with:
    - 0-cells = `Stuff`, `Geom`, `Info`
    - 1-cells = matter/energy flows, curvature, entanglement
    - 2-cells = conservation laws & symmetry constraints

Basically: build the thing you’re asking for, but explicitly labeled so it stays compatible with real physics.

**Examples:**

- [allowed vs disallowed states (Pauli exclusion)](#constraint-relationships "symmetric:constrains")
- kinematically permitted vs forbidden decays

**Why it’s distinct:**
These are relationships defined in [modal logic terms](#modal-relationships "meta:corresponds_to"), not physics dynamics.

---

# ⭐ **3. Counterfactual Relationships** {#counterfactual-relationships}

**Definition:** [“If X had been different, Y would follow.”](#counterfactual-relationships "meta:corresponds_to")

**Examples:**
- gravitational lensing paths if mass were changed
- quantum counterfactual reasoning (e.g., two-slit with detectors off)

**Why it’s distinct:**
These describe [relationships in hypothetical space](#counterfactual-relationships "meta:corresponds_to"), not physical space.

---

# ⭐ **4. Identity / Equivalence Relationships** {#identity-relationships}

**Definition:** [When things are treated as identical under a mapping](#identity-relationships "ontological:defines").

**Examples:**
- gauge equivalence
- diffeomorphism equivalence
- “different coordinates, same geometry”

**Why distinct:**
It’s not symmetry — it’s [quotienting out physical redundancies](#identity-relationships "ontological:defines").

This is a very [category-theory-flavored relationship](#identity-relationships "meta:corresponds_to").

---

# ⭐ **5. Measurement / Observational Relationships** {#measurement-relationships}

**Definition:** [How an observer’s state defines the measurable properties of a system](#measurement-relationships "epistemic:measures").

**Examples:**
- [relativity: time dilation depends on observer’s frame](#measurement-relationships "epistemic:measures")
- [quantum: measurement basis defines outcomes](#measurement-relationships "epistemic:measures")
- horizon effects (Unruh effect)

**Why distinct:**
It’s a relationship between systems and the [observational interface](#measurement-relationships "epistemic:measures").

---

# ⭐ **6. Computational / Algorithmic Relationships** {#computational-relationships}

**Definition:** [State-to-state mappings realized as information-processing or rules](#computational-relationships "meta:corresponds_to").

**Examples:**

- [renormalization group flows](#rg-flows "meta:corresponds_to") as algorithms
- [cellular automaton interpretations](#ca-interpretations "meta:corresponds_to")
- [path integral summation rules](#path-integrals "dynamical:transforms_to")

**Why distinct:**

This is the [link](#phys-comp-link "meta:corresponds_to") between physical state transitions and computation.

---

# ⭐ **7. Optimality / Variational Relationships** {#optimality-section}

**Definition:** Systems [follow paths](#variational-paths "dynamical:transforms_to") that [minimize or extremize](#optimization "symmetric:constrains") some quantity.

**Examples:**

- [least action principle](#least-action "symmetric:constrains")
- [geodesics](#geodesics "geometric:precedes")
- [minimal surface solutions](#minimal-surfaces "symmetric:constrains") in GR
- [optimal control](#optimal-control "symmetric:constrains") in physics

**Why distinct:**

They describe relationships not as forces, but as [optimization constraints](#opt-constraints "symmetric:constrains").

---

# ⭐ **8. Teleological / Goal-State Relationships** {#teleological-section}

Not “purpose” in human terms, but:

**Definition:** Relationships [defined by](#teleological-def "ontological:defines") [end-state constraints](#end-state "symmetric:constrains").

**Examples:**

- [boundary conditions](#boundary-conditions "symmetric:constrains") determining wave solutions
- [final-state constraints](#final-state "symmetric:constrains") in certain QFT formalisms
- [holographic screen boundary conditions](#holographic-boundary "geometric:embedded_in")

**Why distinct:**

[Reverse causality](#reverse-causality "geometric:causes") or [boundary-conditioned behavior](#boundary-behavior "symmetric:constrains") appears in many theories.

---

# ⭐ **9. Meta-Relational / Functorial Relationships** {#meta-relational-section}

**Definition:** [Relationships between](#meta-rel-def "meta:corresponds_to") kinds of relationships.

**Examples:**

- [mapping an interaction relationship](#gauge-gravity "meta:corresponds_to") to a geometric one (as in gauge-gravity duality)
- [renormalization](#renormalization-functor "meta:corresponds_to") as a functor between energy scales
- [category-theoretic transformations](#category-transform "meta:corresponds_to")

**Why distinct:**

This is “relationship-of-relationships,” a [higher-order layer](#meta-layer "meta:emerges_from").

---

# ⭐ **10. Self-Referential / Recursive Relationships** {#recursive-section}

**Definition:** A system’s [structure or rules](#recursive-rules "ontological:composes") [depend on](#self-dependence "dynamical:interacts_with") its own state.

**Examples:**

- [curvature affecting itself](#self-curvature "dynamical:interacts_with") in strong-gravity regimes
- [network models with feedback loops](#feedback-loops "dynamical:interacts_with")
- [RG fixed points](#rg-fixed-points "symmetric:constrains")

**Why distinct:**

This is [recursion](#recursion "meta:emerges_from") — the next layer up from [dynamical relationships](#cat-dynamical "meta:emerges_from").
